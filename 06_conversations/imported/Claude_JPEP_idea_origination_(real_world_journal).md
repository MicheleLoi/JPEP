# **Why This Journal Exists: A Justification**

## The Problem

Academic publishing currently faces an incoherent response to AI assistance:

**Requirement 1: Disclose AI use**  
Most journals now mandate transparency about AI contributions to written work. This seems reasonable—readers deserve to know how something was produced.

**Requirement 2: Don't use AI substantially**  
The same journals that demand disclosure make such disclosure nearly career-ending. Work with significant AI involvement is rejected not because the arguments are weak, but because the process violated emerging norms.

**Result:** A system that rewards dishonesty and punishes transparency.

## The Alleged Justification: Costly Signaling

The strongest defense of current barriers goes roughly:

_"AI makes writing nearly costless. Without barriers, we'd be flooded with low-quality work. Requiring human rewriting imposes costs that filter for genuine intellectual commitment. This protects the signal value of publication."_

## Why This Defense Fails

**1. The signal is unverifiable**  
No one actually checks whether authors rewrote AI drafts or just claimed they did. An unverifiable costly signal is no signal at all.

**2. The threshold is arbitrary**  
Is 60% AI-written acceptable? 40%? 20%? Different journals, different standards. Different reviewers, different intuitions. Arbitrary barriers cannot perform filtering functions.

**3. We already accept low-cost assistance**  
Academic writing routinely involves:

- Research assistants who do literature reviews
- Statisticians who run analyses
- Editors who restructure arguments
- Translators who rewrite in different languages

If costly signaling mattered, we abandoned it long ago. The PI who employs an RA pays money, not time. Why is that acceptable but AI assistance isn't?

**4. It screens for the wrong thing**  
Current norms filter for:

- Willingness to hide AI use, or
- Time spent on performative rewriting

Neither correlates with argument quality or intellectual contribution.

**5. Better signals exist**  
We already have mechanisms to evaluate understanding:

- Peer review questions
- Revision rounds
- Conference Q&A
- Response to criticism

These work regardless of who typed the original draft.

## What Should We Do Instead?

**Judge the argument, not its genesis.**

When Scholar A develops a strong philosophical argument through dialogue with Claude, the question should be: "Is this argument interesting and sound?" Not: "Did Scholar A personally type each sentence?"

If Scholar A can defend the argument, respond to objections, and extend it under questioning, then Scholar A understands it. The intellectual labor has been done, regardless of whether the initial formulation was human-typed or AI-generated.

## The Role of This Journal

_Unlaundered_ exists to test whether the academic community actually cares about genesis or quality.

If scholars read work published here and find it valuable, they will cite it. If they don't, it will be ignored—exactly as weak work in traditional journals is ignored.

The difference: we're not pretending the AI wasn't involved. We're seeing whether that matters.

## This Is Not About Lowering Standards

Let me be clear: I am the editor, and I will reject most submissions. This journal does not publish "anything AI-generated." It publishes **good arguments that happen to be AI-assisted.**

The standard is intellectual merit, not conventional process.

If this experiment succeeds, it proves current barriers serve gatekeeping rather than quality-control functions. If it fails, it proves scholars genuinely care about genesis more than content—which would be an interesting finding in itself.

Either way, we learn something about what academic publishing is actually for.

## IX. Critical Limitations and Open Questions

### Limitation 1: Selection Bias

The venue will attract authors already comfortable with transparency. This makes it difficult to generalize findings about "what's possible" with AI assistance, since we're sampling from the willing rather than representative scholars.

**Mitigation**: This is acknowledged limitation, not fatal flaw. The venue still generates valuable information about upper bound of quality achievable with disclosed AI work.

### Limitation 2: Definition of "Substantial" AI Involvement

What counts as substantial enough to require this venue rather than traditional publication? The venue does not enforce sharp threshold, instead allowing authors to self-determine. ### Limitation 3: Prompt Quality Assessment Standards

Review B1 requires assessing "intrinsic prompt quality." What are the criteria? The venue begins without established standards, developing them through practice. This means early publications may be more exploratory regarding what constitutes "good" prompting.

### Open Question 1: Evolution of AI Systems

**Possible approach**: Review B2 specifies AI system used, treating reproducibility as time-bound. This creates historical record of what was possible when, rather than timeless reproducibility.

Can this mechanism scale beyond philosophy? Different fields have different norms around:

- What counts as "original contribution"
- Role of methodology vs results
- Reproducibility expectations

The split-review mechanism might need field-specific adaptation.

### Open Question 3: Long-term Trajectory

If the venue succeeds (maintains quality, generates engagement), does it:

- Remain permanently discontinuous from traditional systems?
- Eventually become integrated as norms change?
- Create pressure for traditional venues to modify practices?

The answer affects what "success" means and how to evaluate outcomes.
