# Epistemic Trace: From Two Routes to Two Orders — The Authenticity Argument Development

## 1. Starting Question

The user raised a question about the ethical dimension of Section 3's double-contestation structure: **why is self-expression relevant *ethically*?** The current draft (v2) ran two parallel "routes" — a meta-ethical route (expressivism) and an ethical route (the Socratic/Kierkegaardian/Nietzschean authenticity tradition) — but the ethical route lacked independent ethical grounding. It described a philosophical tradition without articulating why that tradition generates an ethical demand for transparency. Could an ethics of authenticity be introduced without relying on Taylor?

## 2. Williams on Integrity

Bernard Williams's notion of integrity was identified as the primary ethical grounding. The key concept: **ground projects** — commitments constitutive of your identity as an agent. A philosopher's mode of inquiry (how they think, what methods they employ, how they relate to their work) is plausibly among their ground projects. When that mode changes significantly through AI assistance, transparency is required to maintain the coherence of these commitments before a community that has reason to care about them.

Critical constraint identified during discussion: integrity in Williams's technical sense concerns identity-constituting commitments, not reputation. The formulation "your integrity is at stake in the community's eyes" risks collapsing integrity into reputation — this must be avoided. The social dimension is real (you maintain legibility of your commitments before others), but the grounding is in the structure of agency, not in how you are perceived.

## 3. Sartre on Bad Faith — Naive Version and Its Three Vulnerabilities

The Sartrean bad faith argument was explored as a second ethical grounding. The naive version: using AI to do philosophy for you is bad faith — a denial of your freedom and responsibility as a thinker.

Three vulnerabilities were identified:

1. **Self-direction problem.** Bad faith in Sartre is primarily a relation of self-deception. The naive version is primarily self-directed (you are deceiving yourself about your own practice) rather than other-directed. But transparency is a social obligation — it concerns what you owe to readers, reviewers, and the community.

2. **Capacity presupposition.** The naive version requires the philosopher to have the capacity for genuine philosophical thinking without AI. This is both empirically uncertain and philosophically questionable — it presupposes an answer to questions about what philosophical thinking essentially is.

3. **Authenticity presupposition.** The naive version presupposes a clear answer to what "authentic philosophical thinking" looks like — precisely the question that the essential contestedness argument shows cannot be settled.

## 4. The Refined Construal

The user developed a refined construal that avoids all three vulnerabilities:

> **The bad faith is not using AI to do philosophy. It is treating AI-assisted philosophical production as "just ordinary business" — denying that it raises questions requiring engagement with others.**

The freedom is genuine: the author exercises real creative freedom in building AI-assisted philosophical architectures. The bad faith is the self-deception that this freedom carries no social obligation — that there is nothing new to disclose, nothing that the community has reason to know about.

This construal avoids the three vulnerabilities:
- It is **socially directed** (the denial concerns obligations to others, not just self-deception about one's own practice)
- It does not **require capacity claims** (the point is not that you could have done it without AI, but that you deny the relevance of the question)
- It does not **presuppose what authentic philosophy looks like** (the point is that you deny others the ability to assess your practice against their own conception)

## 5. The Key Insight Connecting Sartre to the Philosophical Tradition

The user articulated an insight connecting the Sartrean analysis to the first-order philosophical conceptions:

> The existence and prominence of Socrates, Nietzsche, Kierkegaard as canonical figures shows that "philosophy is just pursuing truth through argument" is not the only legitimate option. If it were, there would be no problem of communicating to others, because expectations would be fixed. Denying transparency is like denying that there are other ways of being philosophers — denying the freedom of philosophy itself.

This connects the Sartrean bad faith to essential contestedness: the bad faith consists specifically in closing off the plurality that essential contestedness identifies. It is not just epistemically unjustified (you cannot know that output-evaluation suffices); it is ethically culpable (you are denying that the personal/existential conception is even a legitimate option).

## 6. The Reconceived Two-Order Structure

The user proposed reconceiving the double contestation as a two-order structure:

### First order (two positive conceptions resisting output-only evaluation)
1. **Expressivism (meta-ethical):** If ethics does not track moral facts, outputs cannot be evaluated by correspondence to truth. Process matters.
2. **The personal/existential view (philosophical practice):** Philosophical inquiry is constitutively tied to the inquirer. Outputs cannot be evaluated independently of who produced them and how.

These are logically independent objections to the same target (the cognitivist output-only view). One is a metaethical thesis; the other is a thesis about philosophical practice. Both are present in the philosophical canon.

### Second order (two arguments about why the plurality demands transparency)
1. **Essential contestedness (Gallie):** Because these conceptions are genuinely contested and no one can prejudge which is correct, you cannot silently impose the output-only view. Transparency is required so each position can assess work on its own terms.
2. **Sartrean bad faith (refined construal):** Denying transparency is denying that one's philosophical practice is free, and that this freedom entails accountability to others who conceive of philosophy differently. This is ethically culpable, not just epistemically mistaken.

Williams on integrity then specifies the social/relational dimension: your ground projects include your mode of inquiry; transparency is how you honor their coherence when that mode has changed.

## 7. Opus Assessment (Summary)

A separate Opus instance assessed the restructuring against five questions and approved it. Key findings:

1. **Structural improvement:** The restructuring resolves a real asymmetry in the v2 framing. Expressivism is a metaethical thesis; the personal view is a thesis about philosophical practice — different kinds of claims that are now correctly placed at the same level rather than both being called "routes."

2. **Essential contestedness role clarified:** In the old structure, essential contestedness was doing double duty (framing concept and deployed argument). The new structure assigns it a single, clean role: the primary second-order argument.

3. **Argumentative distribution:** The cognitivist objection is defeated at the first order (the two conceptions show that output-evaluation is not the only option). It is converted to a positive transparency obligation at the second order (essential contestedness + bad faith). This is a cleaner distribution of labor.

4. **Recommendation on Sartre:** Present essential contestedness as the primary second-order argument; the bad faith construal as a one-paragraph deepening, not a parallel pillar. In a word-limited paper, the Sartrean argument needs more setup than it may have space for as a standalone position.

5. **Williams caution:** Use integrity in his technical sense (ground projects, identity-constituting commitments), not as reputation or community standing. The social dimension is real but must be grounded in the structure of agency.

## 8. Decision

Based on the philosophical development and the Opus assessment:

- **Sartre:** presented as a one-paragraph deepening of essential contestedness, not a parallel pillar. The paragraph builds directly from essential contestedness ("silently assuming the output-only view is not merely epistemically unjustified — it is a form of bad faith...").
- **Williams:** one paragraph using integrity in the technical sense (ground projects). Social dimension specified without collapsing into reputation.
- **Structure:** two-order framing replaces two-route framing. First-order parallel is explicitly marked as two logically independent conceptions. Second-order parallel is essential contestedness (primary) + bad faith (deepening).
