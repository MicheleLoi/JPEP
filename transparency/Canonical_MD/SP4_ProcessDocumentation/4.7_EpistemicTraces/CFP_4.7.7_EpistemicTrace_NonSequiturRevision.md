## Key Decisions

### Decision 1: Cut the first component/step of the cognitivist-objection reply

**What existed:** The reply to the cognitivist objection had a "first component" (Introduction) / "first step" (Section 3):
- Introduction: "There is no moral truth-meter. Assessing a philosophical argument requires attending to whether the right considerations were identified and the right sensitivities exercised..."
- Section 3: "...thinking quality is itself part of what ethical scholarship is evaluated for (cf. Section 6.1)."

**User's judgment:** Non sequitur. Confirmed by Opus structural review.

**Opus diagnosis (verbatim key points):**
- The cognitivist objection turns on the context-of-discovery/justification distinction — not on whether we have a moral truth-meter. The objection claims output-level criteria (validity, soundness, whether right considerations are identified) are sufficient. The first component's "no moral truth-meter" addresses a different question (epistemic access to moral reality).
- A cognitivist would simply reply: all those things you describe as "process-dependent" (identifying right considerations, exercising moral sensitivity) are assessable by reading the paper — they are output-level criteria.
- The essentially-contested-concept argument (second component/step) is self-sufficient. It says: the objection presupposes that output-evaluation criteria are settled, but this is precisely what is most contested. The objection is question-begging.
- The "two components" / "two steps" framing was structural deadwood created by the first component's existence. Without it, the structure is cleaner.

**Decision:** Cut entirely. The instinct behind the first component (that process matters to evaluation in ethics) is not wrong — but it belongs in Section 6.1 where the thinking-quality argument is properly developed, not as a step in the cognitivist-objection reply.

**Implication for all planning:** The cognitivist-objection defeat in all planning documents, drafting instructions, and future sections rests solely on the essentially-contested-concept argument. Do not reintroduce the first component in any form.
