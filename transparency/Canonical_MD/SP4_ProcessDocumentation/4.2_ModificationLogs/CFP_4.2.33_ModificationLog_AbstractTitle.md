### MOD-002 — Title decided

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Date | 2026-05-12 |
| Type | Strategic Reorientation |

**Issue Identified:**
The CFP-track work plan called for a title signaling "transparency + AI-assisted ethics research + methodology." Five initial candidates (sober/conventional) were presented; user requested more bite, and three further candidates were added. User then proposed a "Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus" structure: a playful hook upfront, a CFP-anchoring subtitle after "Or." The wink draws on the project's own name (JPEP = Journal Paper on Ethics and AI-assisted Philosophy) reread as "Journal of Prompt Engineered (Moral) Philosophy" — turning the project's working title into a self-aware framing for the paper that asks what such a journal would actually have to require.

**User Feedback/Decision:**
> "The Journal of Prompt Engineered (Moral) Philosophy. Or [you add]"
>
> "no, start with that; add the CFP relevant bit after OR"
>
> "C"

**Resolution:**
Final title: *The Journal of Prompt Engineered (Moral) Philosophy: Or, Why AI-Assisted Ethics Research Requires Process Transparency*. The hook ("Prompt Engineered (Moral) Philosophy") signals the topic with self-aware wit; the subtitle is argumentative rather than descriptive (names the question the paper answers, not the answer itself), aligning with §3's dialectical strategy. The title is saved in the same file as the Abstract (`CFP_5.4.15_Abstract_v1.md`); no separate artifact created.

**Rationale:**
The user rejected the more sober variants (A: framework-named; B: agent-integrity foregrounded; D: documentation-adequacy foregrounded) in favor of the argumentative C. Reading: the title should name the contested question the paper takes up, not pre-announce its positive contribution; the agent-integrity grounding can do its work inside the paper rather than in the title slot. The "Or" subtitle convention also gives the title a quasi-period-philosophical register that matches the paper's use of the canonical tradition (Socrates, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Williams, Sartre) — the title performs the same kind of move the paper performs philosophically: pairing the contemporary AI question with a recognizable scholarly form.
