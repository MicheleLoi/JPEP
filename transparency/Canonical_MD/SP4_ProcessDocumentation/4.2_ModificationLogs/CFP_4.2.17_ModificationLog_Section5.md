### MOD-001

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Date | 2026-03-17 |
| Type | Strategic Reorientation |

**Issue Identified:**

The JPEP v1 Section 5 derives three principles—ecological validity, good faith orientation, and cost structure through costly signaling—from *discontinuity from prestige systems*: the argument is that a venue that opts out of traditional academic metrics creates design space for these principles, which would be difficult to implement within credential-bearing structures. This derivation is venue-specific and tied to a journal-creation proposal. The CFP version has no journal proposal. The source derivation therefore cannot transfer.

**User Feedback/Decision:**

> "let's proceed to 5"

(Direction to adapt Section 5 per the work plan, which specifies: "rewrite derivation, same principles." See `CFP_5.3.1_WorkPlan_CFP_Adaptation.md` Section C.)

**Resolution:**

Complete rewrite of the derivation. The three principles are re-derived as normative conditions that current compliance-based disclosure mandates fail to meet:

- *Ecological validity*: current mandates impose disclosure obligations without creating disclosure conditions — documentation is appended retrospectively in formats not designed for process capture, structurally enabling all four mechanisms identified in Section 2.
- *Good faith orientation*: current mandates are adversarially structured — they require transparency under conditions where transparency is penalized, generating the Goodhart dynamic (Strathern 1997) that burdens honest scholars while failing against sophisticated evasion.
- *Cost structure / costly signaling*: current mandates create false equivalence between nominal and substantive disclosure — a one-sentence acknowledgment satisfies the same formal requirement as comprehensive process documentation, collapsing the evidentiary value of disclosure.

Section 5 is renamed from "Signaling Discontinuity from Prestige System" to "Conditions for Adequate Transparency." A closing subsection (5.4) explicitly names all three conditions and bridges to Section 6, preparing Section 6.1's back-reference ("the three principles from Section 5").

**Rationale:**

The work plan (Phase 2 decisions, MEMORY.md) classified Section 5 as "rewrite derivation, same principles." The three principles are argumentatively sound and transferable; only their derivation was venue-specific. Re-deriving them from the failure of compliance-based mandates creates a tighter logical sequence: Section 2 diagnoses the failure → Section 5 derives what an adequate framework must provide → Section 6 delivers the framework. The section becomes a genuine diagnostic bridge rather than a discussion of institutional strategy.

**User Feedback/Decision:**

> "refine"

(User instruction to apply Reviewer B's recommended refinements.)

**Resolution:**

Two targeted edits:

- **5.3**: Added qualification after the asymmetry claim: "The asymmetry is not absolute; AI tools can themselves assist in generating plausible-seeming documentation. But documentation specific enough to enable genuine tracing assessment is substantially harder to fabricate than to produce authentically, and the cost differential makes honest participation the path of least resistance."
- **5.2**: Removed one restatement iteration. The "reframing" sentence ("A transparency framework oriented toward good faith asks not 'how do we detect underreporting?' but...") was cut; the preceding claim ("Closing those ambiguities requires philosophical specification, not surveillance") and the following practical implication ("Requirements that scholars committed to transparency find reasonable...") were retained as the tighter two-move version.

**Rationale:**

The fabrication qualification is philosophically important: the cost-structure argument should acknowledge its own limits to be credible. The 5.2 trim improves concision without argumentative loss — the two remaining moves (specification over surveillance; requirements that produce better documentation) cover the ground of the three.
