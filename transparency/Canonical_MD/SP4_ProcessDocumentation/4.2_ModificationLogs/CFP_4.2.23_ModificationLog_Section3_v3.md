# Modification Log: Section 3 v2 → v3 (Two-Order Restructuring)

## What v2 Contained

Section 3 v2 (~1,350 words) structured the double-contestation argument as two parallel "routes":
- **Meta-ethical route (expressivism):** ethical inquiry's contested nature at the metaethical level — whether ethics tracks truth or serves expressive/constructive functions
- **Ethical route (authenticity tradition):** the Socrates/Kierkegaard/Nietzsche tradition treating philosophical activity as constitutively self-expressive

Both routes were presented within a single "Ethical Inquiry as Essentially Contested" section. The personal/existential tradition appeared as a "second level" of contestation appended to the first, rather than as an independent first-order conception. Essential contestedness functioned both as a framing concept and as the deployed argument, creating ambiguity about its role.

The section also contained: the nature-of-activities problem (Santoni de Sio et al. 2016), the cognitivist objection and its defeat, the qualification about simple applied ethics, and the pivot to tracking.

## What Changed in v3

### 1. Two-order restructuring (primary change)

The two-route parallel was replaced with a two-order structure:

**First order — two positive conceptions resisting output-only evaluation:**
- Expressivism (metaethical thesis): if ethics does not track moral facts, outputs cannot be evaluated by correspondence to truth
- The personal/existential view (thesis about philosophical practice): inquiry is constitutively tied to the inquirer; outputs cannot be evaluated independently of who produced them and how

These are now presented as logically independent objections to the same target. The section explicitly notes that one is a metaethical thesis and the other is a thesis about practice — different kinds of claims, now correctly at the same level.

**Second order — two arguments about why the plurality demands transparency:**
- Essential contestedness (Gallie): because the conceptions are genuinely contested, no one can silently impose the output-only view (primary argument)
- Sartrean bad faith (refined construal): denying transparency is denying the freedom of philosophy itself (one-paragraph deepening)

### 2. Personal/existential view developed as first-order conception

The Socrates/Kierkegaard/Nietzsche material was moved from its former position as a "second level" within essential contestedness to a standalone first-order conception parallel to expressivism. New material added: the argument that the canonical status of these thinkers shows that "philosophy is just truth-tracking through argument" is not the only legitimate option — which means expectations are not fixed, which means communication about process is required.

### 3. Sartrean bad faith added as one-paragraph deepening

A dense paragraph following the essential contestedness argument introduces Sartre's bad faith in a refined construal: the bad faith is not using AI to do philosophy; it is treating AI-assisted philosophical production as "just ordinary business" — denying that it raises questions requiring engagement with others. This builds directly from essential contestedness (moving from "epistemically unjustified" to "ethically culpable") without requiring its own apparatus.

### 4. Williams integrity paragraph added

A paragraph introducing Bernard Williams's notion of integrity in its technical sense: ground projects (identity-constituting commitments) include one's mode of philosophical inquiry. Transparency is how you honor the coherence of those commitments before a community that has reason to care about them. Explicit guard against collapsing integrity into reputation.

### 5. New references added

- Sartre, J.-P. (1956). *Being and Nothingness*.
- Williams, B. (1981). "Persons, character and morality." In *Moral Luck*.

### 6. What was preserved

- Opening (practice changing / continuous vs. discontinuous question)
- Nature-of-activities problem (Santoni de Sio et al.)
- Cognitivist objection and its defeat (question-begging argument)
- Qualification about simple applied ethics
- Pivot to tracking
- Closing paragraph on visibility and documentation

## Why the Changes Were Made

The v2 structure had an asymmetry: expressivism is a metaethical thesis while the personal/existential tradition is a thesis about philosophical practice. Placing both as "routes" obscured their logical independence and different kinds of contribution to the argument. The two-order restructuring places them correctly: both resist output-only evaluation (first order), and both motivate transparency through essential contestedness (second order).

The personal/existential view lacked independent ethical grounding in v2. Williams on integrity and Sartre on bad faith supply this: Williams specifies the social dimension (what you owe the community regarding your identity-constituting commitments), and Sartre specifies the ethical culpability of denying the plurality (not just epistemically unjustified but a form of self-deception about freedom).

Essential contestedness was doing double duty in v2 (framing concept and deployed argument). The two-order structure assigns it a single clean role: the primary second-order argument for why the first-order plurality demands transparency.
