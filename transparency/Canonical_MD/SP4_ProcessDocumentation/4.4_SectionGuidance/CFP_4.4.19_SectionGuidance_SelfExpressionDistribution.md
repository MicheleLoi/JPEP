# Section Guidance: Self-Expression/Authenticity Argument — Co-Equal Distribution

## Purpose

This document specifies how to integrate the self-expression/authenticity argument across all sections of the CFP adaptation paper. **This is not a subordinate enrichment or capstone.** The authenticity argument has co-equal weight with the tracking argument and must be developed in parallel throughout the paper, wherever the tracking argument appears in detail.

**User directive (SID-20260401-140255):** "I want the authenticity argument to have the same weight as the expressivist one and to be developed in parallel. Where the expressivist argument appears in detail the authenticity one goes too. [...] you are definitively allowed, even more, encouraged, to make radical changes to the existing drafts, while you should exploit the parallel to the argument scheme developed for expressivism."

## The Architectural Insight: One Argument, Two Levels

This is **NOT** two parallel arguments that happen to converge. It is **one argument** — essential contestation — applied at **two levels**:

### Level 1 — Meta-ethical (already in the paper):

```
What IS ethical inquiry?
  → cognitivism vs. expressivism vs. constructivism
  → we cannot settle this
  → output-evaluation criteria are themselves contested
  → therefore we must TRACK what ethics is becoming
  → documentation enables tracking
```

### Level 2 — Ethical (to be added with equal weight):

```
What does DOING ethical inquiry REQUIRE of the inquirer?
  → authenticity? truthfulness? only output quality?
  → we cannot settle this either
  → the second dispute inherits the structure of the first
  → therefore we must enable each tradition to assess the work on its own terms
  → documentation enables authenticity assessment
```

### Convergence (structural, not coincidental):

Both levels flow from the same feature of ethical inquiry — its essential contestedness. Both lead to the same practical requirement: comprehensive process documentation. Opacity defeats both purposes simultaneously. The convergence is not a rhetorical bonus; it is architecturally entailed.

**Name this the "double contestation" throughout the paper.**

## Philosophers and Artistic Parallels

**DO NOT USE Taylor.** The user explicitly dislikes Taylor. All references to Taylor (1991) from prior design artifacts must be replaced.

### The Tradition: Socrates, Nietzsche, Kierkegaard

**Socrates** — the unexamined life is not worth living (*Apology* 38a). Philosophy as self-examination, not just doctrine-production. The practice of philosophy reveals and constitutes the philosopher. The Socratic reader asks: has the inquirer examined their own practice?

**Nietzsche** — "It has gradually become clear to me what every great philosophy up till now has consisted of — namely, the confession of its originator, and a species of involuntary and unconscious autobiography" (*Beyond Good and Evil* §6, Kaufmann translation). What a philosopher chooses to investigate, how they respond, what they pursue — this IS the confession. The Nietzschean reader asks: is this text confession or imposture?

**Kierkegaard** — truth as subjectivity (*Concluding Unscientific Postscript*): the *how* of relating to truth matters constitutively, not just the *what*. Indirect communication: the form of philosophical expression is not separable from its content. The detached system-builder who does not exist in their own thought has already failed. The Kierkegaardian reader asks: does the author's *how* constitute genuine engagement?

### Artistic Parallels

**Architect/bricks** — An architect does not lay bricks; the building is no less expressive of architectural thought. What expresses the self is the vision that organizes, not the manual execution of each element.

**Harold Cohen / AARON** — Cohen designed AARON's aesthetic principles, selected its outputs, and curated its development over decades. The resulting works expressed Cohen's artistic vision precisely because the *structure of decisions* remained his, even as execution was delegated. This is the generative-art precedent for creative delegation that remains genuinely self-expressive.

### The Citation Precedent

Philosophy already treats certain process details as self-expressive. Williams's engagement with Greek tragedy, Cavell's pairing of ordinary language philosophy with film criticism, Nozick's deployment of decision theory in ethics, Lewis's bridge-building between modal logic and metaphysics — each citation pattern constitutes an implicit methodological proposal about what resources matter for philosophy. The community reads these patterns as expressive of intellectual identity: they tell you who the philosopher is, not just what sources they consulted.

This precedent is crucial: **the self-expression argument does not introduce a foreign demand on philosophical practice.** It extends an already-accepted norm. Philosophy has always treated certain process details — which intellectual resources the author engages with — as identity-constitutive. AI usage is a new dimension of the same practice. Whether a philosopher arrives at an insight through engaging with Kierkegaard or through prompting an AI reveals something about their mode of intellectual engagement — what they find worth thinking with, how they relate to their own work. The *choice of interlocutor* — human source or AI — is itself a self-expressive act.

The self-expressive function operates at the level of *patterns*, not individual citations or prompts. Just as a single citation is functional but a citation pattern is a confession, a single AI prompt is instrumental but an overall mode of AI engagement expresses intellectual character.

**Source:** Reinterpretation of the citation-patterns passage in v1/v2 Section 6.1 (`Paper/MDversion/06_mandatory_transparency_in_practice.md`). The original passage framed citation patterns epistemically (you need to know whose thought you're following to learn from the example). The deeper point: citation patterns are already a form of Nietzsche's "unconscious autobiography" — and AI usage type is another dimension of the same self-disclosure.

#### Change 1: Add Level 2 contestation to "Ethical Inquiry as Essentially Contested" subsection

**Insertion point:** After the paragraph ending "The question 'Does AI change philosophy?' presupposes an answer to a question that admits of no neutral adjudication." (This is where the meta-ethical contestation has been fully stated.)

**Function:** Extend the Gallie argument from one level to two. The same essential contestedness that applies to what ethical inquiry IS also applies to what DOING it REQUIRES of the inquirer. This is not a new argument — it is the same argument applied reflexively.

**Draft passage (~150 words):**

> The contestation extends further. We disagree not only about what ethical inquiry *is* but about what engaging in it ethically demands of the inquirer. A philosophical tradition running from Socrates through Kierkegaard and Nietzsche treats philosophical activity as constitutively self-expressive: Socrates held that the unexamined life is not worth living, treating philosophy as a discipline of self-knowledge rather than a body of conclusions (*Apology* 38a); Kierkegaard insisted that the *how* of relating to truth matters constitutively — the detached system-builder who does not exist in their own thought has already failed (*Concluding Unscientific Postscript*); Nietzsche described every great philosophy as "the confession of its originator, and a species of involuntary and unconscious autobiography" (*Beyond Good and Evil* §6). On these accounts, the process by which philosophical conclusions are reached is not separable from their intellectual standing. Whether this tradition is correct — whether authentic personal engagement is constitutive, whether only the quality of resulting arguments matters, or whether some intermediate position holds — is not a dispute we can settle independently of the first-level contestation. The second inherits the structure of the first. Call this the double contestation.

#### Change 2: Add Level 2 derivation to "From Answer to Tracking" subsection

**Insertion point:** After the paragraph deriving the tracking response from Level 1 contestation (ending approximately "If the question of what ethical inquiry is cannot be answered, what can be done?" and the subsequent tracking proposal).

**Function:** Derive the authenticity response from Level 2, using the same logical move. If we cannot settle what ethical authoring requires, we should not assume the question away.

**Draft passage (~120 words):**

> The same logic applies at the second level. If we cannot settle what ethical authoring requires of the author, we should not assume the question away. We should create conditions under which each position can assess the work on its own terms. Documentation is the feasibility condition: without it, the Socratic reader cannot evaluate whether the inquirer has examined their own practice, the Nietzschean cannot distinguish confession from imposture, the Kierkegaardian cannot assess whether the author's *how* constitutes genuine engagement. With documentation, each can. This demand is not foreign to philosophical practice: philosophy already treats citation patterns as expressive of intellectual identity — the extension to AI-assisted production is a new dimension of the same norm. The double contestation thus generates a single practical requirement from two independent routes — and documentation satisfies both.

#### Change 3: Expand "The Stakes" closing

**Current text ends:** "They do not presuppose an answer to the question of what ethical inquiry is. They create the conditions under which that question can be productively addressed."

**Replacement (expand to cover both levels, ~80 words):**

> They do not presuppose an answer to the question of what ethical inquiry is, nor to the question of what ethical authoring requires of the author. They create conditions under which both questions can be productively addressed. The double contestation means the paper's practical proposal — comprehensive process documentation — serves two independent purposes that no single tradition can dismiss without begging a question that remains genuinely open.

**Estimated addition to Section 3:** ~270-370 words. New total: ~1,620-1,720 words.

### SECTION 6 — §6.1 REWRITE FROM SCRATCH

**Priority: SECOND.** This is where the full self-expression argument is developed, in parallel with the tracking argument.

**Current state:** `CFP_5.4.8_Section6_v3.md` (~1,520 words). §6.1 "From Conditions to Practice" (~650 words) contains: two routes to process-dependency → epistemic virtue passage → MHC framework → tracing condition.

**Output file:** `CFP_5.4.8_Section6_v4.md`

**Action:** Rewrite §6.1 from scratch. §6.2, §6.3, §6.4 remain unchanged (check for consistency).

#### New §6.1 Structure

**1. Opening — restate the double contestation (~80 words):**

Restate from Section 3: ethical inquiry is essentially contested at two levels. Both levels require process documentation. This section shows how a single framework serves both.

**2. The tracking route (~200 words):**

Compress the current two-routes argument. For non-cognitivists, quality criteria are constitutively process-dependent (non-cognitivist coherence is coherence of a *person's* evaluative attitudes; particularist sensitivity requires genuine attunement; constructivist fidelity demands actual deliberation). For cognitivists, essential contestedness means no tradition can foreclose assessment by those whose criteria are process-dependent. Process documentation is required on both accounts. This is the Level 1 derivation, already in the paper — compress, do not repeat in full.

**3. The authenticity route (~300 words):**

This is the core new content. Develop the full self-expression argument:

a. **The tradition applied to AI:** The tradition named in Section 3 (Socrates, Nietzsche, Kierkegaard) generates an apparent objection to AI-assisted philosophical work. If philosophy is self-expression, delegation to an AI introduces alien agency into a process whose value lies in disclosing the self. Call this the romantic objection. (~50 words)

b. **Its defeat — architect/bricks and Cohen/AARON:** The romantic objection confuses the medium of expression with its locus. What expresses the self is not the manual production of sentences but the vision that organizes them — the questions selected, the risks taken, the intellectual judgments brought to the whole. An architect does not lay bricks; the building is no less expressive. Harold Cohen's AARON demonstrates this in generative art: Cohen designed the aesthetic principles, selected outputs, curated development over decades — the *structure of decisions* remained his, even as execution was delegated. (~90 words)

c. **The citation precedent — self-expression is already a philosophical norm:** Philosophy already recognises process details as self-expressive. Citation patterns — Williams's engagement with Greek tragedy, Cavell's pairing of ordinary language with film — constitute implicit methodological proposals about what resources matter for philosophy. The community reads these patterns as expressive of intellectual identity. AI usage extends this: whether a philosopher arrives at an insight through engaging with a human source or through AI dialogue reveals something about their intellectual mode of engagement — the choice of interlocutor is itself a self-expressive act. The self-expression argument does not introduce a foreign demand; it extends a norm philosophy already accepts. (~90 words)

d. **What different modes of AI engagement express:** Cautious use expresses caution. Concealed use expresses opacity — not intellectual character but its erasure. Documented delegation expresses willingness to test a new medium, commitment to making the process visible. In Nietzsche's terms, the documented author is the most legible Nietzschean confessor — the documented process discloses intellectual character: what the author notices, pursues, where they override the AI, where they follow it. (~70 words)

e. **Opacity as imposture; transparency as condition:** Concealment misrepresents the process; the text performs a philosophical identity not the author's own — the opposite of confession. Documentation restores the conditions for self-expression. The vulnerability of disclosure — making visible uncertainties, iterations, moments where AI output moved ahead of authorial understanding — is what makes the process genuine confession rather than curated narrative. This vulnerability is continuous with the intellectual vulnerability philosophy has always valued. (~70 words)

**4. Convergence (~80 words):**

The conditions enabling tracking (transparency, documentation, process visibility) are the same conditions enabling self-expression under AI assistance. This convergence is structural: both requirements flow from the same feature of ethical inquiry — its essential contestedness at multiple levels. Opacity defeats both simultaneously. A transparency framework does not constrain authorial identity; it is what makes authorial identity legible in a context where the production process is no longer self-evident from the text alone.

**5. MHC framework (~250 words):**

Retain the existing MHC content (Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven) but add one crucial observation: the tracing condition — that the directing person *understood* what was being produced and *endorses* it as their own intellectual contribution — IS the authenticity condition. Tracing operationalizes what the philosophical tradition demands. The question "did the author understand and endorse?" is precisely the question Kierkegaard's truth-as-subjectivity makes constitutive and that Nietzsche's confession metaphor requires. Tracing is where the two levels of the argument converge operationally. The three conditions from Section 5 implement these requirements.

**Target §6.1 length:** ~1,000-1,100 words. **Section 6 total:** ~1,870-2,020 words.

**Implementation note:** The existing epistemic virtue paragraph ("Full process disclosure is itself an expression of epistemic virtue. The vulnerability it entails...") should NOT be kept as a separate observation. Its content is absorbed into §6.1.3e — the vulnerability-as-confession point. Do not duplicate.

#### Conclusion Structure

1. **Recapitulation** (~100 words): Brief summary of the paper's argument. Essential contestation of ethical inquiry at two levels generates a single practical requirement: comprehensive process documentation. The framework proposed in Section 6 and assessed in Section 7 serves both levels — tracking what ethics is becoming and enabling each tradition to assess the authenticity of the process.

2. **The convergence** (~200 words): Develop the convergence point. The conditions enabling tracking are the same conditions enabling authentic self-expression. This is not coincidental but structurally entailed: both requirements flow from the same underlying feature (essential contestedness). The tracing condition (Section 6) operationalizes both — "understood and endorsed" is simultaneously the epistemic standard for tracking and the authenticity standard the philosophical tradition demands. A framework satisfying both is more robust than one satisfying either alone, because its justification does not depend on resolving either level of contestation.

3. **Self-exemplification** (~250 words): The paper's documentation apparatus (SP-1 through SP-5) is simultaneously: (a) a tracking instrument, and (b) a form of philosophical self-expression — a record of intellectual character showing what the author chose to investigate, where they followed the AI, where they overrode it, what they found worth documenting. The self-philology of Section 7 — applying philological method to one's own production process — is Socratic self-examination transposed into a new medium. **Self-referential acknowledgment (per CFP_4.7.8):** The paper's transparency record is a partial instance, not fully solved. The v1/v2 phase was documented retrospectively, the CFP phase prospectively. This limitation is itself instructive: contemporaneous documentation enables both tracking and authentic self-expression; retrospective reconstruction is possible but costly and incomplete. The three criteria (attribution, trajectory, understanding-and-endorsement) must be satisfied contemporaneously to serve both purposes fully.

4. **Forward look** (~150 words): Community assessment mechanisms remain to be developed. The double contestation means the community of legitimate evaluators is broader than any single tradition can specify — it includes those for whom only argument quality matters and those for whom the author's mode of engagement is epistemically relevant. The framework must serve all of them. Early experimentation shapes norms; the paper offers one implementation for assessment.
