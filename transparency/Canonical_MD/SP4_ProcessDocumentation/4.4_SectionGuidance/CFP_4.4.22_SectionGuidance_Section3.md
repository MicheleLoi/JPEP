# Section Guidance: §3 — Why Engage Transparently with AI-Assisted Ethics Research?

## Status

**Current authoritative draft:** `CFP_5.4.4_Section3_v3.md` (filename retained from the v3 era; in-place git versioning since 2026-05-12; frontmatter `version:` field is the source of truth for the version label).

**Word count:** ~2,470 words (CFP v5).

**Section guidance word-count target:** ~2,400–2,500 words. This is a substantial expansion from the original III-era target of 800–1,000 words. The expansion is earned: §3 carries the paper's core argumentative load — defeating two truth-tracking framings, defeating one welfare-economic framing, grounding the transparency duty in agent-integrity, and setting up the visibility argument. It is not a survey section; it is the section where the paper does its philosophical work.

## Architecture (Post-2026-05-12)

§3 is structured as a sequence of argumentative moves, each in its own subsection or paragraph cluster:

1. **Opening** (no header). Empirical observation that AI is changing ethics research practice. Continuous-vs-discontinuous question. We cannot answer this directly — the difficulty is conceptual.
2. **The Nature-of-Activities Problem.** Constitutive vs. regulative rules (Santoni de Sio, Faber, Savulescu, and Vincent 2016). The question of whether AI changes ethical inquiry cannot be answered without settling what ethical inquiry is.
3. **Two Conceptions That Resist Output-Only Evaluation.** Expressivism (metaethical thesis) and the personal/existential view (thesis about practice). Logically independent; both present in the philosophical canon (Enoch, Shafer-Landau, Gibbard, Blackburn; Plato, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche).
4. **Ethical Inquiry as Essentially Contested.** Gallie's framework applied. Sartrean bad faith deepening (refined construal: bad faith is not using AI but treating its use as "just ordinary business"). Williams integrity (ground-projects, *not* methodological integrity — see "Hard Constraints" below). Cordasco's welfare-economic challenge steelmanned + answered three ways (welfare-on-welfare reply; register reply; institutional concession).
5. **Why Output-Evaluation Fails in Ethics.** Defeats the cognitivist objection — one truth-tracking framing of AI transparency.
6. **Reproducibility Is Not the Issue.** Defeats the second truth-tracking framing — science's methodological-reproducibility model — and explicitly grounds the transparency duty in agent-integrity (not methodological-soundness).
7. **From Answer to Tracking.** Pivot to the framework specified in §4–§6.
8. **The Disruption of Implicit Process Signals.** Closing subsection: AI severs the text–agent link; what was implicit must now be explicit. Exemplars: Nozick, Parfit, Williams, Cavell.

## Hard Constraints

These are load-bearing for the paper's whole argument. Any revision of §3 must preserve them.

1. **Integrity is agent-integrity, not methodological integrity.** Williams's notion of ground-projects (identity-constituting commitments) is the only sense of "integrity" §3 trades on. Any framing that lets a reader read the transparency duty as "methodological soundness" or "scientific rigor" or "quality control" must be corrected. Confirmed 2026-05-12; this is the load-bearing distinction for the paper's whole transparency argument.

2. **No truth-conducive philosophical-values list.** Phrases like "intellectual honesty, methodological self-consciousness, guided thought" smuggle in truth-tracking framings the section rejects. If a positive characterization of what's at stake in the implicit signals is needed, use *agent-presence* language (the philosopher's mode of engagement, the marks of an agent at work), not values-as-truth-conducive-virtues.

3. **The transparency duty is a moral duty, not a post-institutional duty.** Confirmed 2026-05-12 (v4). Institutional encodings of the duty (journal policies, disclosure regimes, mandates) are downstream; the duty itself is grounded in the inquirer's commitments. Welfare-economic considerations apply to the encoding question, not to the duty.

4. **Reproducibility does not transfer from science to philosophy.** Confirmed 2026-05-12 (v5). Any AI-transparency argument that proceeds from "science demands reproducibility, so philosophy should too" is making a category mistake: philosophy's evidence is the argument itself; there is no method to reproduce.

5. **Cognitivism is not asserted as a premise.** Confirmed earlier (v3 architecture). The cognitivism / non-cognitivism dispute is used as an *illustration* of essential contestedness, not as a metaethical thesis the paper endorses.

6. **Section numbering: pre-renaming.** §3 uses pre-renaming numbering per the 2026-04-09 renaming key in `adapt.md`. All section cross-references in §3 ("§4", "§5", "§6") use pre-renaming labels — they refer to the old §5/§6/§7 = new §4/§5/§6.

## Exemplars: Who Qualifies?

Implicit-signal exemplars (in the closing visibility subsection) must be *agent-signatures* — distinctive ways particular philosophers stand in relation to their work — rather than *instrumental methodological moves* serving truth-tracking.

**Current list (v5):**

- **Nozick** — decision-theoretic reasoning motivating libertarian conclusions. Accepted as a Nozickian signature (his philosophical personality is in the choice).
- **Parfit** — objections-as-discovery; working-through transparency that displays a mind encountering and working through difficulties in real time. Accepted as a Parfittian signature.
- **Williams** — Greek tragedy in dialogue with contemporary moral philosophy. Accepted as a Williamsian signature (his bridge between ethics and literature is part of who he is as a philosopher).
- **Cavell** — ordinary-language philosophy paired with film criticism. Accepted as a Cavellian signature (cultural-objects-as-philosophical-resources is a Cavellian move).

**Rejected:**

- **Lewis** — modal logic / metaphysics bridge. Rejected as instrumental methodology: Lewis used modal logic as a tool for tracking metaphysical truths, not as a Lewisian signature in the same sense the others above operate. Decided 2026-05-12.

When considering future additions to this list, apply the same distinction: agent-signature (in) vs. instrumental methodology (out).

## What §3 Does Not Do

- Does not specify the framework. That is §5's job (Mandatory Transparency in Practice).
- Does not specify community-assessment mechanisms. That is §6's job (Community Assessment of Documentation Adequacy).
- Does not argue against AI use. The duty argued for is a duty of *transparency* given AI use, not a duty against using AI.
- Does not generalize to disciplines other than ethical inquiry. Generalization to other humanistic disciplines is acknowledged as out of scope (per `CFP_5.3.27` O2 reply: the user does not claim knowledge of other disciplines sufficient to take that responsibility).
- Does not adjudicate the cognitivism / non-cognitivism dispute. It is used as illustration of contestedness, not resolved.
- Does not claim that philosophy fails to aim at truth. The reproducibility disanalogy is "philosophy's evidence is the argument itself, so reproducibility doesn't apply *even if* philosophy is truth-tracking." Truth-tracking is left open.
