# Section Guidance: §6.4 — Gaming-Defense Absorption

## Status

**Current authoritative draft:** `Paper/MDversion/CFP_FullPaper_v1.md` §6.4 (in-place; v1.8 after this revision).

**Word-count target:** ~1,050w (current 702w in all of §6 + ~350w added gaming-defense block in §6.4 specifically). §6.4 grows from 3 paragraphs to 5.

## Rationale for Placement

The argument relocated here is about *how evaluators encounter documentation under assessment*. §6.4 is the assessment-norms section. The argument's central insight — that no single normative target can defeat fabrication, only evaluative diversity can — answers a question the framework's design itself cannot answer ("won't your SP-1–SP-5 just create a richer target to game?"). That question is properly raised when the assessment apparatus is being characterized, i.e. here, not before §5 has introduced the framework being assessed.

## Architecture (Post-Absorption)

§6.4 now opens with the gaming-defense block, then continues with the existing calibration / cost-objection / self-exemplification material.

1. **Para 1 (NEW, ~175w) — "Specificity is necessary but insufficient."**
   - Open by tying back to §6.3's dual-assessment structure rather than re-introducing the assessment topic.
   - The Strathern-mechanism (a measure becoming a target ceases to function) extends from existing mandates to any single-target regime — including this paper's own framework.
   - The **fabrication-vs-vagueness distinction** (load-bearing): specificity closes vagueness-gaming but cannot close fabrication-gaming. AI systems can generate process documentation that performs compliance with whatever the accepted model is — the right frequency of human interventions, the right pattern of override decisions, the right density of independent intellectual contribution — without any of these reflecting the actual process.
   - Land on: "What prevents this second failure is the absence of a single normative target against which fabrication can be optimized." (Anchor sentence from pre-revision line 209.)
2. **Para 2 (NEW, ~175w) — "Evaluative diversity as defense-in-depth, conditional on community good faith."**
   - Reference §3.3's essential-contestedness work; do not re-derive it. Acceptable opener: "Section 3 argued that essential contestedness is an epistemic fact about ethical inquiry. It has a structural consequence for assessment practice."
   - Evaluators approach documentation from diverse and mutually irreducible perspectives. Documentation that performs execution-level engagement to satisfy one tradition may fail tests another tradition applies; documentation performing the right kind of self-expressive vulnerability may lack the inferential rigor a cognitivist demands.
   - The **defense-in-depth** label (load-bearing — verbatim preservation required): "Diversity of legitimate evaluative perspectives functions as something analogous to what information security calls defense in depth: no single fabrication strategy can optimize against all assessment criteria at once, because the criteria are not reducible to a common metric."
   - Community-condition rider: the defense holds only if evaluative diversity is maintained in practice. If one conception of authentic human authorship achieves de facto dominance through professional incentive structures or hiring practices, the diversity collapses in practice even if it persists in principle. Assessment in good faith — encountering documentation on its own terms rather than against a template — is the disposition that prevents this collapse.
3. **Para 3 (REWRITTEN, ~155w) — Calibration.** Current §6.4 paragraph 1 (line 315 in pre-revision file) opens with "The good faith orientation from Section 4 shapes documentation assessment." Replace with a direct transition: "Within these norms, calibration matters." Then keep the existing content: depth of review proportional to claimed contribution; AI-generated-central-insight requires more sustained tracing than structuring-a-well-understood-argument.
4. **Para 4 (UNCHANGED, ~75w) — Cost-objection rebuttal.** Current §6.4 paragraph 2 (line 317): documentation requirements re-impose missing metacognitive scaffolding; Zimmerman 2002, Cheng et al. 2025. No edit.
5. **Para 5 (UNCHANGED, ~125w) — Self-exemplification.** Current §6.4 paragraph 3 (line 319): the article's archive evidences *feasibility* but not *adequacy*. No edit.

## Hard Constraints

1. **Fabrication-vs-vagueness distinction is the load-bearing claim** without which §5's framework reads as a richer target to game. Para 1 must contain both (a) the Strathern-extension move and (b) the fabrication-vs-vagueness move. If only one fits the word budget, expand the budget; the framework's structural defense requires both.

2. **"Defense in depth" must survive verbatim.** It is the only handle readers can carry forward; cutting or paraphrasing it leaves the structural claim without a name. The information-security analogy is acceptable to keep or trim, but the label itself is non-negotiable.

3. **Do not re-derive essential contestedness.** §3.3 establishes it; §6.4 applies it. The opener "Section 3 argued that essential contestedness is an epistemic fact… it has a structural consequence for assessment practice" carries the work without redundancy.

4. **Drop "from Section 4."** The relocated argument originated as §4.2's second half; in the new structure, §4 no longer hosts a "good faith orientation" condition. References to "the good faith orientation from Section 4" must be removed. Where good faith reappears (Para 2 community-condition; Para 3 calibration framing), introduce it locally as the assessment disposition this section is characterizing.

5. **Do not cut the cost-objection rebuttal or self-exemplification.** These are pre-existing §6.4 content with their own reasons for being. The absorption adds before them; it does not replace them.

## What §6.4 Does Not Do

- **Does not re-introduce "ecological validity."** That label is cut from the paper.
- **Does not state the framework's design conditions.** §5 is the framework; §6 is its assessment. The gaming-defense argument here is about why this *kind of assessment* is necessary, not what the framework needs to be.
- **Does not develop the bad-faith parallel to §3.3.** Pre-collapse §4.2 closed with a paragraph drawing the community-level / individual-level parallel ("good faith at the community level is the practical correlate of essential contestedness at the conceptual level"). This is cut from the relocation. §7's monitoring sentence carries the parallel implicitly; reinstating it here would re-bloat the section.
