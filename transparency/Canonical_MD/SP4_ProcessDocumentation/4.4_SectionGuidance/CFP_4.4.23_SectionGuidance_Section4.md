# Section Guidance: §4 — Why Existing AI-Disclosure Formats Don't Fit Philosophy

## Status

**Current authoritative draft:** `Paper/MDversion/CFP_FullPaper_v1.md` §4 (in-place; v1.8 after this revision).

**Word-count target:** ~640w (target band 580–720w). Previous v1.7 §4 was 2,089w. The compression cuts the two-conditions scaffolding (ecological validity + good faith), relocates the gaming-defense argument to §6.4, and retains only the Hosseini-Resnik-Holmes critique with light trimming.

## Architecture (Post-Collapse)

§4 does one thing: contest the most influential alternative in the AI-disclosure literature on grounds specific to philosophy.

1. **Opening (~75w).** The Hosseini, Resnik & Holmes (2023) three-location prescription — disclose AI use in (a) the main text, typically methods or introduction, (b) the references, and (c) supplementary materials. Increasingly adopted by major journals including some in philosophy. The opening states the prescription and the disciplinary stakes.
2. **The critique (~470w, three moves).**
   - **Move 1 — Methods sections.** The convention against methods sections in philosophy is constitutive, not a gap. It expresses the view that method is continuous with argument. Requiring a methods section answers, by formatting fiat, the contested question whether philosophical method is detachable from the argument that exhibits it.
   - **Move 2 — References and supplementary.** Listing AI tools among references treats them as interlocutors on a par with cited authors. Disclosing prompts and outputs in supplementary materials presupposes that AI's contribution is isolable as discrete inputs and outputs — a presupposition that fits AI as a query-retrieval tool but breaks down once AI participates in ideation.
   - **Move 3 — Self-defeat (the novel contribution).** Applied selectively to AI-using papers, the prescription is structurally inconsistent with §2's diagnosis. An AI-using submission required to add a methods section, list AI tools in references, and append prompt logs is visibly distinct from comparable non-AI submissions. This is not a neutral transparency signal but a marker of formal deviance — a disclosure penalty compounded on top of the reputational penalty §2 documents. The author who complies is doubly penalized; the author who minimizes compliance games the very mandate meant to discipline them.
3. **Closing synthesis (~95w).** Numbered recap of the three moves: the prescription (i) prejudges contested questions about what philosophy is, (ii) creates new disclosure penalties of exactly the kind §2 diagnoses, and (iii) violates an ecological-practice condition the framework must satisfy. Frame the section's argumentative scope: the §5 framework is more demanding than the three-location prescription, not less. This is an argument that *disclosure format matters*. The framework is a first iteration offered for community refinement, not a claim of unique necessity.

## Hard Constraints

These are load-bearing for §4's compressed form. Any revision must preserve them.

1. **Move 3 (self-defeat / visible-anomaly) is the section's distinctive contribution.** It must remain near-verbatim from the pre-collapse §4.3 paragraph at line 229. The transparency-paradox callback to §2 (BaHammam 2025; cf. Schilke & Reimann 2025) is part of this move; do not cut.

2. **The methods-section argument is intricate; preserve the disciplinary-self-understanding move.** "A reader of a Kantian or Wittgensteinian or contemporary analytic paper is meant to follow the method *in* the argument; the prose is the method." This sentence anchors Move 1 and must survive the merge of pre-collapse lines 225 + 227. Acceptable to trim restatement around it; not acceptable to cut it.

3. **The numbered (i)/(ii)/(iii) recap in the closing paragraph is load-bearing.** Downstream readers and (in future revisions) reviewers anchor the critique by this enumeration. Drop the (i)/(ii)/(iii) numbering *inside* development paragraphs (they were doing organizational scaffolding that the new structure makes redundant), but keep them in the synthesis sentence.

4. **No two-conditions framing.** The phrases "ecological validity" and "good faith orientation" are deliberately removed. Move 3's recap references "an ecological-practice condition" as a paraphrase only — do not reintroduce the label.

5. **Section title is provisional.** The current §4 heading "Conditions for Adequate Transparency" no longer fits. Replace with a title naming what the section actually does. Provisional: "Why Existing AI-Disclosure Formats Don't Fit Philosophy." Alternatives to consider at execution: "Against the Three-Location Prescription"; "The Philosophical Inadequacy of Existing AI-Disclosure Formats." Decision to be recorded in MOD-015.

## What §4 Does Not Do

- **Does not state framework conditions.** Pre-collapse §4 named two normative conditions (ecological validity, good faith orientation) that any adequate framework must satisfy. The new §4 abandons this framing. Conditions, if needed, are stated where they bite — the framework's own characterization in §5, the assessment apparatus in §6, the conclusion's monitoring claim in §7.

- **Does not develop the gaming-defense argument.** The Strathern-mechanism / fabrication-vs-vagueness / defense-in-depth / community-condition chain has been relocated to §6.4 (see CFP_4.4.24). It belongs there because it is an argument about how *evaluators* encounter documentation under assessment — not about adequacy-conditions on the framework's design.

- **Does not pre-defend the §5 framework.** Pre-collapse §4.3 included a sentence marketing the SP-1–SP-5 architecture as preserving comprehensive documentation while keeping it separable from the paper's conventional form. §5 itself makes this point. The closing of §4 stops at the meta-level claim (disclosure format matters) and leaves the framework's positive characterization to §5.

- **Does not recapitulate §3.** Pre-collapse §4.3 ended with a ~120w recap of §3's essential-contestedness route (meta-ethical + ethical convergence). §5.1 already does this operationally ("did the author understand and endorse this?"). Do not reintroduce the recap.
