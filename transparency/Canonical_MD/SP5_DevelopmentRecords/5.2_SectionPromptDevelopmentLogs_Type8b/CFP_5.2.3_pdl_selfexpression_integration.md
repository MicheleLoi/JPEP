### PDL-006: User proposes combined Gallie + authenticity move

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Date | 2026-04-01 |
| Session | SID-20260401-111336 |
| Authored by | User |

**The move:** Don't commit to authenticity ethics being right. Don't commit to expressivism being right. Apply the Gallie move *reflexively* — to the practice of philosophical ethics authoring itself, not just its content.

Authoring philosophical ethics is essentially contested at two levels:
1. **Metaethical:** what are we doing? (pursuing truth, expressing emotions, constructing norms — the cognitivism/non-cognitivism dispute already in the paper)
2. **Ethical:** what does it mean to engage in the practice of philosophical ethics *ethically*? (is authenticity required? sincerity? does only output quality matter?)

We don't know which views are correct. We disagree. The argument is not that Taylor is right, or that expressivism is right. It is that *because* we disagree, we should enable people holding those different perspectives to satisfy their claims on the work — provided it is feasible and not too costly.

Transparency/documentation is the feasibility condition: it makes it *possible* for each perspective to evaluate the work on its own terms. The virtue ethicist can assess character-in-action. The expressivist can assess authentic engagement. The cognitivist can assess reasoning quality. Without documentation, none of them can do so.

This is the Gallie move applied reflexively: the same argument that says "we can't prejudge ethical outputs, so we must track" now says "we can't prejudge what ethical authoring requires, so we must create conditions for assessment."

### PDL-007: Opus — reflexive Gallie analysis; draft Bridge A paragraph

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Date | 2026-04-01 |
| Session | SID-20260401-111336 |
| Produced by | Opus (first principles) |

**Finding:** The reflexive Gallie move is stronger than all prior options. It renders Taylor and Williams structurally optional — they become illustrations of one contested position rather than load-bearing premises.

**The argument structure:**

The cognitivism/non-cognitivism dispute (already established in the paper) generates a *second-order* dispute about what ethical authoring requires of the author. If cognitivism is correct, the author's authenticity is not specially relevant — what matters is the quality of argument. If expressivism or constructivism is correct, the author's authentic engagement with the material becomes epistemically significant. These are not independent disputes; the second inherits the structure of the first.

Because both disputes are essentially contested, we cannot stipulate that one tradition's demands on the author are the ones that matter. The response is not to resolve the contest but to create conditions under which each position can assess the work. Documentation is the feasibility condition: without it, the expressivist cannot distinguish genuine engagement from imposture, the virtue ethicist cannot evaluate attentiveness and character-in-action, and the cognitivist cannot trace argument development. With it, each can.

Taylor and Williams now function as *named instances* of the contested position (expressivism/authenticity) — not as the argument's foundation. The argument does not depend on their being right.

**Why this is ethics-specific:**

The cognitivism/non-cognitivism dispute is peculiar to ethics: it concerns the *ontological status of what ethical inquiry is producing*. A contested methodology in history or physics does not generate second-order disagreement about whether the inquirer's authentic engagement is part of what's being assessed. In ethics it does, because some positions hold that the inquirer's practical stance is partly constitutive of the inquiry's output.

**Draft Bridge A paragraph (164 words):** for insertion in CFP_4.7.11, between "the third author is the one whose philosophy is most legibly a confession" and "The philosophical hinge is transparency":

> But the force of this concern does not depend on Taylor or Williams being right. The practice of philosophical ethics is essentially contested at two levels: we disagree about what ethical inquiry *is* (the metaethical dispute between cognitivism, expressivism, and constructivism that generates the problem of this paper), and we disagree about what engaging in that inquiry *ethically* demands of the inquirer — whether authenticity is required (Taylor 1991), whether truthfulness is the operative virtue (Williams 2002), or whether only the quality of the resulting arguments matters. These are not independent disputes; the second inherits the structure of the first. Because we cannot settle what ethical authoring requires of the author, we should not assume the question away. We should instead create conditions under which each position can assess the work on its own terms. Documentation is the feasibility condition for that assessment: without it, the virtue ethicist cannot evaluate attentiveness, the expressivist cannot distinguish genuine engagement from imposture, and the cognitivist cannot trace the trajectory of argument development. With it, each can.

### PDL-010: Citation patterns as self-expression — new architectural ingredient

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| Authored by | User (insight) + Opus (integration into guidance) |

**User insight:** The v1/v2 Section 6.1 citation-patterns passage ("Williams's engagement with Greek tragedy, Cavell's pairing of ordinary language philosophy with film criticism... each citation pattern constitutes an implicit methodological proposal about what resources matter for philosophy") was originally framed epistemically — you need to know whose thought you're following to learn from the example. User now recognises the deeper point: citation patterns are *self-expressive*. They are a form of Nietzsche's "unconscious autobiography" — they tell you who the philosopher is as an intellectual.

**The analogy:** AI usage type is analogous to citation patterns. Just as which sources a philosopher engages with expresses their intellectual identity (commitments, traditions, what they find worth thinking with), *how* they engage with AI — cautiously, experimentally, with documentation, concealed — expresses the same thing. Furthermore, *whether* the author arrives at an insight through a human source or through AI dialogue is itself a self-expressive choice: the choice of interlocutor reveals something about the philosopher's mode of intellectual engagement.

**Architectural consequence:** The self-expression argument is not introducing a foreign demand on philosophical practice. It *extends an already-accepted norm*. This makes the argument substantially less vulnerable to the objection that authenticity requirements are an exotic imposition. Philosophy already cares about process-as-self-expression (via citation). AI documentation is the continuation of that norm under new production conditions.

**Integration into CFP_4.4.19:**
1. New top-level subsection "The Citation Precedent" added to "Philosophers and Artistic Parallels" — establishes the precedent architecturally alongside the tradition (Socrates/Nietzsche/Kierkegaard) and artistic parallels (architect/bricks, Cohen/AARON).
2. New point (c) in §6.1 authenticity route: "The citation precedent — self-expression is already a philosophical norm" (~90 words), inserted between the defeat of the romantic objection (b) and modes of AI engagement (d, formerly c).
3. Brief seed in Section 3 Change 2 (From Answer to Tracking): one sentence noting that the documentation demand extends a self-expressive practice philosophy already recognises in citation.
4. Word counts updated: §6.1 target +~100 words; total net addition ~1,200-1,550 words.

**Risk identified (Risk #8):** Citation precedent could be overstated — most individual citations are functional, not self-expressive. Mitigation: pitch the argument at the *pattern* level throughout. A single citation is functional; a citation pattern is a confession. Same for AI: a single prompt is instrumental; an overall mode of engagement is self-expressive.
