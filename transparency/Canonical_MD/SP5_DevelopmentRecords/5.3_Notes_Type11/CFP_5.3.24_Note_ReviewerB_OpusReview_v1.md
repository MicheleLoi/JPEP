## 1. Fit with the CFP

**Addressed well:** Methodological implications — transparency framework is genuinely responsive.

**Addressed obliquely:**
- What tasks can/cannot be supported by AI? — presupposed, never analyzed explicitly
- Implications for ethics as a field? — Section 7.4 gestures at this, underdeveloped

**Not addressed:**
- Which uses involve special wrongs or risks? — entirely absent
- Discovery/justification distinction — dismissed in 6.1 too quickly; exactly what the CFP asks

**Verdict:** Relevant but narrower than the CFP invites. Answers "how to document" but not "should AI be used in ethics and how."

---

## 2. Core Argument Assessment

**Chain:** Essential contestedness → output-evaluation is process-dependent in ethics → tracking requirement → framework

**Strengths:**
- First link (essential contestedness → process-dependence) is the paper's best philosophical contribution
- Distinction between tracking and mere disclosure is useful and clearly drawn

**Weaknesses:**

1. **Proves too much** — if output-evaluation in ethics is always process-dependent, the argument applies to *all* ethics papers, not just AI-assisted ones. Paper never explains why AI specifically triggers the requirement rather than making feasible a requirement that was always there.

2. **Comparison cases too easy** — contrasts ethics with mathematics/experimental science only. What about history, literary criticism, political theory? If argument generalizes to all humanistic scholarship, framing is misleadingly narrow.

3. **Move from "process matters" to "this specific framework" is underdetermined** — derivation gestured at rather than argued. Why these three conditions? Why these five layers rather than a detailed methodological section in the paper?

4. **Costly signaling self-undermining** — paper acknowledges setup costs decrease over time (6.4), which weakens costly signaling as the framework succeeds and becomes standard.

5. **Circularity** — paper argues for transparency framework then cites its own implementation as evidence. Implementation not independently assessed. Conflates feasibility and adequacy.

## 4. Conclusion Assessment

**Problems:**
- Structural: summary, not a conclusion. Should draw out implications, acknowledge limitations, reframe contribution.
- Self-referential paragraph makes claims not earned: SP-1–SP-5 as "tracking instrument" asserted not demonstrated.
- **Socratic analogy is overreaching:**
  - Socratic examination is dialogical; documentation is monological — different epistemic structures
  - Socratic examination aims at self-knowledge; documentation aims at accountability — different goals
  - "Mandatory transparency functions as philosophical self-examination" conflates external accountability with internal examination
- Final sentence ("recoverable record of intellectual agency") substitutes rhetoric for argument

**Recommendation:** Rewrite entirely. Drop or substantially qualify the Socratic analogy. Acknowledge limitations. End with claims proportionate to what has been established.

---

## 5. What's Missing

1. Engagement with risks/wrongs of AI use in ethics (even if transparent)
2. Serious engagement with discovery/justification distinction
3. Abstract
4. Counterexamples to essential contestedness argument (settled applied ethics cases?)
