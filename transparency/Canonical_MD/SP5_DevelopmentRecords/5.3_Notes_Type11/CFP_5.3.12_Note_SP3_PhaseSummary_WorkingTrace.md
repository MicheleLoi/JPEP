## Note on method: hubs as reconstruction infrastructure

**Relevant for SP-3 decisions:** (1) whether to introduce hubs as an artifact type in the SP-3 material; (2) whether hubs enable AI-equipped reviewers to check hypotheses about the development record.

### How hubs are being used here

The phase reconstruction above relies heavily on the `_HUBS/` folder. The hub files (`CHAT_<UUID>.md`) serve as index nodes that link session UUIDs to the artifacts produced in each session. In practice, the reconstruction proceeds as follows:

1. A frontmatter field in an SP4/SP5 artifact contains a `source_chat_id` (UUID) or `session_id` (SID).
2. The corresponding hub file is read to retrieve: the session's date, its human-readable name, and the full list of artifacts it produced.
3. This allows cross-artifact dating even when individual artifact frontmatter lacks a `date` field — the hub supplies the date indirectly via the session record.

Example from this session: `4.2.9` had no `date` field. Its `phase1_chat_id` (`3b4ee4d7`) was traced to the session "JPEP section 8 writing" dated Oct 15, 2025 — resolving the question of whether Section VIII existed before the Oct 19 Appendix session.

Without hubs, this reconstruction would require reading body text for date clues or inferring from logical ordering alone — both much slower and less reliable.

### Implication for SP-3 artifact introduction

Hubs are currently an infrastructure artifact generated by a script (`obsidian_connections_with_chat_hubs.py`), not a manually authored document type. They are not listed in the current SP-1 through SP-5 ontology. The question for SP-3 is: should they be introduced as a named artifact type?

Arguments for introducing them:
- They are the primary mechanism by which session identity is attached to artifacts (session → artifact linkage)
- They make the documentation record machine-traversable: a reader (human or AI) can walk from any artifact to its origin session and then to all sibling artifacts from that session
- Their absence is already felt: artifacts with no hub (orphans) are genuinely harder to date and contextualise

Arguments for not introducing them as a first-class type:
- They are derived artifacts (generated from frontmatter, not authored); introducing them risks implying they carry the same epistemic weight as authored traces
- SP-3 is already introducing SP-1/2/3 as new document types; adding hubs may overload the reader

**Suggested framing for SP-3:** Introduce hubs as a *navigation layer* distinct from the documentation layer. They do not document thinking — they index sessions. Their role is infrastructural: they make the documentation record traversable. This distinction preserves the epistemic hierarchy (authored traces > generated indices) while acknowledging that the index is what makes the record usable.

### Implication for AI-equipped reviewers

Hubs are especially useful for AI-assisted review because:
- An AI reviewer given access to the `_HUBS/` folder can reconstruct the session sequence independently, without relying on the author's narrative in SP-3
- It can check hypotheses: e.g., "was Section VIII written before the Appendix session?" can be answered by reading `4.2.9` frontmatter → hub → date, without trusting the author's claim
- It can detect inconsistencies: if an artifact claims to have been produced in session X but session X's hub doesn't list it, that is a flag
- It can identify orphans (artifacts with no hub link) as documentation gaps — a form of automated completeness checking

This suggests that hubs, even if not introduced as a first-class document type in SP-3's narrative, should be explicitly mentioned as part of the *review infrastructure* — what an AI-equipped reviewer would use to audit the claims in SP-3 itself.
