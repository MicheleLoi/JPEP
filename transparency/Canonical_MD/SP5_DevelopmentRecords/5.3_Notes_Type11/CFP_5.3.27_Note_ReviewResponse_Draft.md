## Shoulders Objections

### S1 — MHC transfer not argued

The move from autonomous weapons systems to scholarly authorship is asserted ("transfers structurally") without examining which features carry over and which don't. Stakes, reversibility, and the nature of "control" are radically different.

**Reply:**

The fact that the stakes and reversibility are different is immaterial and doesn't grasp the nature of the argument. The argument is not "tracing and tracking are justified for weapons, so they must be necessary here"; nor is it an argument by analogy "tracing and tracking are required for autonomous weapons because they have features xyz; writing papers has features xyz; so they are required here too". The paper provides its own argument why they are required here. It stands on independent ground. What IS common is just the requirement: tracing and tracking. But we don't even need - and it would be confusing to provide - an account of why they are needed there. We're just linking to that concept because we know it exists and we don't need to reinvent the wheel. 


---

### S2 — Question-begging charge is itself question-beggable

The paper charges the cognitivist objection with begging the question, but the paper's own argument presupposes the expressivist/existentialist view is a live option. A cognitivist could level the same charge symmetrically.

**Reply:**

---

This misunderstands the contested concepts argument by Gallies or ignores it. Sure, cognitivists could level that charge, but until they decide to create a separate community they just can't assume they are sourrounded by like minded individuals and they cannot fail to care. The point is not whether expressivist/existenilist philosophies are abstractly "live". The point is that people with those views exist and the community has never decided to expel them (the analytic philosophy community in regards to expressivists; and broader community with existentialists too)

### S4 — Costly signaling undermined by AI

If the very tool being documented can generate detailed-seeming process documentation, fabrication costs are not equivalent to authentic production costs. The paper asserts equivalence without engaging the objection.

**Reply:**

Use appropriate hedging. The harder to track and the more complex a true process, the higher the cost of creating a convincing fake. AI can be used by reviewers to spot inconsistencies. Authors can be asked about inconsistencies. Typically real-world process like this one will have apparent inconsistencies, authors will be asked about (e.g. because the reviewer's AI will spot them). Authors know how to answer. Authors who have not meaningfully controlled the process will not. Or at least that's the conjecture. But generally this argument should be demoted and hedged - the setting should be non-adversarial. The hope is that over time patterns of authentic vs. fake will emerge. And creating authentically looking fakes will be so hard that there will no point in investing computing like that. Try to elaborate on this (requires opus to do well)

## Opus Objections

### O1 — Proves too much

If output-evaluation in ethics is always process-dependent because standards are essentially contested, the argument applies to *all* ethics papers, not just AI-assisted ones. The paper never explains why AI specifically triggers the transparency requirement rather than merely making feasible a requirement that was always there.

**Reply:**

---There is an argument about visibility, that got cut, and has to do with the point in the v1: The venue design actualizes traditional values that opacity under AI production threatens. Philosophy has always valued guided thought—showing readers not just conclusions but reasoning processes. Williams's engagement with Greek tragedy, Cavell's pairing of ordinary language philosophy with film criticism, Nozick's deployment of decision theory in ethics, Lewis's systematic bridge-building between modal logic and metaphysics—each citation pattern constitutes an implicit methodological proposal about what resources matter for philosophy. Philosophy values intellectual honesty: admitting uncertainty, acknowledging objections, revealing limits. It values methodological self-consciousness: Socratic dialogue, phenomenological description, reflective equilibrium matter as contributions.

What I'm trying to saying is that previous philosophical writing was rich of process signals. I've read this and that (yes one could cite without ever reading the book, but there were both social norms against that, i.e. secondary sources should be cited, and also some evidence in terms of depth of analysis). A lot of philosophical reasoning reflected the way an author could persuade himself of something. A lot of writing (think Parfit) really seems the author discovering the problem presenting it and solving it - a reasoning process. Nothing corresponds to that IN THE AUTHOR when the writing is done by LLM. 


### O2 — Comparison cases too easy

The paper only contrasts ethics with mathematics and experimental science. What about history, literary criticism, political theory? If the argument generalizes to all humanistic scholarship, the framing is misleadingly narrow.

**Reply:**

not an objection. I don't know other disciplines enough for taking that responsibility. And this can't be taken an objection. Science and mathematics are relevant because the analogy with them grounds an objection. But it's not immediately plausible for those fields (perhaps with the exception of history, that tends ot be factual).

### O3 — Framework derivation underdetermined

The move from "process matters" to "this specific framework" is gestured at rather than argued. Why these three conditions? Why five documentation layers rather than a detailed methodological section in the paper?

**Reply:**

---

Because a detailed methodological section in the paper alters the way philosophy is written. It rescues transparency by altering the practice immediately to such an extent as to make it unrecognizable (and paradoxically, it would tend to immediately make true one of the claims about the nature of the practice that , without it, remain interestingly open). 



### O4 — Costly signaling self-undermining

The paper acknowledges (§6.4) that setup costs decrease as documentation becomes standard. This weakens costly signaling as the framework succeeds — the paper undercuts its own justification for the mechanism.

**Reply:**

--- The main justification is not costly signalling. The cost remains very high (e.g. computing costs). And this also seems to betray an ambiguity in the costly signalling argument. It's not merely that documenting itself is costly; it's also that the activity being documented is costly. Look at how many changes and interactions, and reviews, and analyses, and choices this paper had. That's the costly thing, that needs being documented. Documenting is costly also because there has to be something substantial to document.
