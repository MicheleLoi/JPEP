## 2. Guidance vs. Reality

### CFP_4.4.19 (Self-Expression Distribution) — the dominant guidance document

This is by far the most consequential guidance file. It specified changes to every section of the paper and dictated the integration of the self-expression/authenticity argument as co-equal with the tracking argument. It specified a precise sequencing plan (Section 7 v2 additions first, then Section 3, then Section 6, then Section 7, then Introduction, then Section 5, then Section 2, then Conclusion).

**Evidence of compliance:**
- Section 3 v2.1 added the double contestation exactly as specified. The Socrates/Kierkegaard/Nietzsche passage closely follows the ~150-word draft in the guidance.
- Section 3 v3 went further than the guidance specified — adding the "Two Conceptions That Resist Output-Only Evaluation" subsection, Sartre's bad faith, and Williams's integrity. These were not in CFP_4.4.19. The guidance specified ~270-370 words of additions to Section 3; v3 added ~150 words net but completely restructured the section's architecture.
- Section 6 v4.1 rewrote 6.1 from scratch as specified. The architect/bricks and Cohen/AARON material appeared as specified. The guidance called for ~1000-1100 words for the new 6.1; the actual 6.1 is approximately that length.
- Section 7 v2 added both additions per CFP_4.4.14 (the earlier guidance document). v3.1 then absorbed the authenticity enrichments per CFP_4.4.19.
- Introduction v2.1 named the double contestation and signaled both levels, as specified.
- Section 5 v2.1 added the brief authenticity notes as specified ("This also serves the authenticity requirement").
- Section 2 v4.1 did NOT add the closing-paragraph change specified in CFP_4.4.19. The guidance specified replacing the closing paragraph with text adding "they also destroy the conditions under which AI-assisted philosophical work can function as genuine intellectual engagement rather than its simulation." The actual v4.1 closes: "The remaining sections undertake that project." The authenticity dimension is absent from Section 2's final version. This is a divergence.

**Evidence of divergence:**
- The Conclusion at ~570 words is well under the 800-1000 word target specified in CFP_4.4.19.
- Section 3 v3 (2026-04-03) restructured the section in ways CFP_4.4.19 (2026-04-01) did not anticipate. The two-order structure (expressivist conception as first-order parallel to personal/existential conception, with essential contestedness operating at a second order) was not in the guidance. This is an evolution beyond the guidance.
- CFP_4.4.19 specified "DO NOT USE Taylor" — no Taylor appears in any draft. Compliance confirmed.
- CFP_4.4.19's Risk 1 warned that "Level 2 treated as subordinate despite equal-weight directive." By v3 of Section 3 and v4.1 of Section 6, the authenticity argument does appear to have approximately equal weight — the risk mitigation worked.

### CFP_4.4.14 (Section 7 Additions) — surgical guidance

This was a precise implementation plan: insert passage A at location X, insert passage B at location Y, add references. Section 7 v2 implemented it verbatim. The Abdulhai et al. passage and the SRL cost-objection reply appear in v2 exactly as drafted in the guidance.

### CFP_4.4.18 (Appendix A v3 Guidance) — not yet implemented

This is a detailed design document for rewriting Appendix A v3. No corresponding draft exists in the 5.4_SectionDrafts folder. The guidance specifies a complete restructuring (A.1 through A.6) replacing the reproduction-test model with documentation-adequacy assessment. It was superseded by the appendix-elimination decision recorded in CFP_5.2.4 (PDL-004), making its detailed specifications moot — though SP-2 and SP-3 inherit some of its design thinking.

### CFP_4.4.20 (SP-3 Guidance) — not yet implemented

This is the most elaborate guidance document in the set — a 285-line design document specifying SP-3 as a full research paper with a philological methodology, three-phase structure, full-corpus reading requirement, and detailed constraints. No SP-3 draft exists yet. The guidance is anticipatory, not reflective of completed work.

### CFP_4.4.15 (V1/V2 Artifact Link Recovery) — metadata infrastructure

This is a cowork plan for browser-based verification of artifact links in v1/v2 modlogs. It does not bear directly on section drafts but documents the metadata infrastructure work enabling graph visualization.

### CFP_4.4.16 (Hypothesis Verification Cowork Prompt) — metadata infrastructure

Same category as 4.4.15: a cowork plan for verifying hypothesized artifact-to-archive-file mappings. Twelve specific hypotheses (H1-H12) with test conditions. Not directly related to section content.

### CFP_4.4.17 (Selected Graph Specifications) — figure design

Specifies criteria for selecting 2-4 subgraphs from the megagraph as paper figures. Each must "make a claim" and be grounded in actual archive data. Relates to Section 7's three criteria (attribution, trajectory, understanding-and-endorsement). Connected to Appendix A v3 design (now partially absorbed into SP-3 guidance).

### Summary: guidance vs. reality

The guidance documents split into two categories. The section-specific guidance (4.4.14 and 4.4.19) drove actual draft revisions and were implemented with high fidelity, though with some divergence in Section 2 and the Conclusion. The infrastructure and future-work guidance (4.4.15, 4.4.16, 4.4.17, 4.4.18, 4.4.20) specify work not yet completed — they are forward-looking design artifacts rather than reflections of completed drafting.

## 3. Cross-Section Interactions

### The cognitivist-objection cut (2026-03-11)

The earliest documented cross-section interaction. The revision_note in CFP_5.4.3_Introduction_v1 records: "First component of cognitivist-objection reply cut on Opus structural review." The same cut is recorded in CFP_5.4.4_Section3_v1 with the identical diagnosis: "Same diagnosis as Introduction: 'thinking quality requires process access' does not answer the context-of-discovery/justification objection." This was a simultaneous revision affecting two sections, driven by a structural review identifying a non-sequitur argument.

### The double-contestation cascade (2026-04-01)

CFP_4.4.19 is explicitly a cascading change document. It specifies modifications to seven sections (Introduction, Sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and Conclusion) all deriving from a single architectural decision: elevating the self-expression/authenticity argument to co-equal status. The sequencing was specified: Section 3 first (establishes the double contestation), Section 6 second (develops the full argument), Section 7 third (enriches assessment criteria), Introduction fourth (signals both levels), Section 5 fifth (brief dual-purpose notes), Section 2 sixth (closing note), Conclusion last.

Evidence of the cascade executing:
- Section 3 v2.1 added the double contestation
- Section 6 v4.1 rewrote 6.1 to develop both routes
- Section 7 v3.1 incorporated authenticity readings of the three assessment questions
- Introduction v2.1 named both levels
- Section 5 v2.1 added the authenticity sentence
- Section 2 v4.1 did NOT add the specified closing paragraph (cascade broke here)
- Conclusion v1.1 is shorter than specified

### The discovery/justification cut and its propagation

Section 6 v1 contained a paragraph beginning "We do not work within the traditional discovery/justification framework (Reichenbach, 1938)." This was cut in v2, with the transformation note recording "Reviewer A verdict (cut discovery/justification paragraph)." This cut is significant because the Introduction v1 used discovery/justification language: "Process transparency confuses the context of discovery with the context of justification." By v2.1, the Introduction retained this language but only as the cognitivist's framing, not the paper's own. The cut in Section 6 made the paper's position consistent: it does not engage in the discovery/justification frame but lets the cognitivist state it and then defeats it through essential contestedness.

### Section 5 as stable bridge

Section 5 is referenced by both Section 2 (which identifies the problem Section 5 diagnoses) and Section 6 (which satisfies Section 5's conditions). Section 5's three conditions (ecological validity, good faith orientation, costly signaling) are cited verbatim in every version of Section 6. The terminology change from "principles" to "conditions" (recorded in Section 6 v2's transformation note) was a harmonization with Section 5. Despite this bridging role, Section 5 itself was barely revised — its stability suggests it was written correctly from the start or that surrounding sections adapted to it rather than the reverse.

### Section 6.2 as invariant core

Within the most-revised section (Section 6), subsection 6.2 — the SP-1 through SP-5 table, the Lloyd engagement, the nested-concerns diagram — survived essentially unchanged across all four versions. The instability was concentrated in 6.1, where the philosophical grounding was repeatedly reworked. This suggests the practical framework was settled early while the philosophical justification for it remained in flux.

### Section 7 as downstream receiver

Section 7 accumulated additions from two separate guidance documents (CFP_4.4.14 for the Abdulhai/SRL additions, CFP_4.4.19 for the authenticity enrichments) without initiating changes in other sections. It is a pure downstream consumer of architectural decisions made in Sections 3 and 6.

## 4. What Got Cut vs. Added

### Major additions across the version chain

1. **The double contestation / self-expression argument.** This is the single largest addition across the entire draft set. It entered Section 3 in v2.1 (2026-04-01) and propagated to all other sections. Source: CFP_4.4.19, itself derived from CFP_4.7.11_EpistemicTrace_SelfExpressionArgument.md and CFP_5.2.3_pdl_selfexpression_integration.md. The philosophical tradition (Socrates, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche) was entirely absent from v1 drafts.

2. **The two conceptions subsection (Section 3 v3).** The expressivist conception and personal/existential conception as parallel first-order arguments against output-only evaluation. This went beyond what CFP_4.4.19 specified.

3. **Sartre's bad faith (Section 3 v3).** Added 2026-04-03. Not specified in any guidance document. Treats silently assuming the output-only view as ethically culpable — "a denial of freedom in the form of self-deception."

4. **Williams's integrity (Section 3 v3).** Ground projects — identity-constituting commitments. Added 2026-04-03. Not specified in any guidance document.

5. **Harold Cohen / AARON (Section 6 v4.1).** Generative art as precedent for creative delegation that remains self-expressive. Also architect/bricks analogy. Specified in CFP_4.4.19. Boden & Edmonds (2009) citation added.

6. **Abdulhai et al. stance-neutralization evidence (Section 7 v2).** 68.9% increase in stance neutralization under LLM assistance. Specified in CFP_4.4.14.

7. **Self-regulated learning cost-objection reply (Section 7 v2).** Zimmerman (2002), Cheng et al. (2025). Documentation as metacognitive monitoring that counteracts cognitive offloading. Specified in CFP_4.4.14.

### Major cuts across the version chain

1. **The "thinking quality" argument (Section 6 v1 to v2).** The passage arguing that "Article evaluation never assessed merely whether arguments are valid. It always also assessed thinking quality" was cut in v2. The transformation note records this was per "Reviewer A verdict." This was a significant argumentative move — the thinking-quality argument was presented as the paper's own position in v1 but was cut entirely rather than being preserved in modified form.

2. **The discovery/justification paragraph (Section 6 v1 to v2).** "We do not work within the traditional discovery/justification framework (Reichenbach, 1938)" — cut. Reichenbach (1938) was removed from the paper's references.

3. **The "we do not" negative paragraphs (Section 6 v1 to v2).** Three paragraphs stating what the paper does NOT argue (not from moral desert, not from economic necessity, not that traditional venues should adopt). Cut in v2 — the transformation note says "positive argument carries the weight."

4. **The Requirement of Visibility subsection (Section 3 v1 to v2.1).** Entire subsection cut. Declared "pure signposting."

5. **The Stakes subsection (Section 3 v1 to v2.1).** Compressed from a full subsection to two sentences merged into "From Answer to Tracking."

6. **Section 6.4 Pilot Observations — version history.** v1 discussed timestamps and "training examples." v3 rewrote this as a "two-layer architecture" (raw transcript + SP-3 synthesis). v4.1 compressed it to a single paragraph, cutting the second paragraph about limitations.

7. **Section 2.3 as a subsection (Section 2 v2 to v3).** "Why Compliance Mandates Cannot Close the Gap" was folded into the close of Section 2.2, eliminating the subsection boundary.

8. **Hedged adoption-data caveat (Section 2 v3 to v4.1).** The qualification "while systematic data on philosophy and ethics specifically is difficult to obtain—partly because disclosure practices are themselves the problem" was shortened to remove the caveat. The redundancy pass treated it as unnecessary hedging.

9. **The reproduction-test development history (Section 7 v1 to v3.1).** v1 had a full paragraph explaining that the reproduction test was proposed and rejected. v3.1 compressed this to one sentence.

### Ideas that appeared and then disappeared

**The "thinking quality" concept.** Present in Section 6 v1 (2026-03-23). This argued that scholarly evaluation always assessed thinking quality — not just validity — and that this was obscured by the discovery/justification distinction. It was cut in v2 (same date). The revision_note says the paragraph was cut per "Reviewer A verdict." The concept does not reappear in any later version. The thinking-quality argument was subsumed by the more fundamental essential-contestedness argument: rather than arguing that we DO assess thinking quality (a positive claim), the paper shifted to arguing that we CANNOT settle what evaluation criteria are correct (a negative claim from which the same conclusion follows).

**The "non-cognitivist coherence / particularist sensitivity / constructivist fidelity" trio.** This appeared in Section 6 v2 as the "two routes" argument: "non-cognitivist coherence is coherence of a person's evaluative attitudes, particularist sensitivity requires genuine perceptual attunement, and constructivist fidelity demands that a reasoning agent actually underwent the deliberative procedure." By v3, this was simplified to expressivism + authenticity tradition. By v4.1, the trio was compressed further under the "meta-ethical route" label. The particularist and constructivist examples were retained in v4.1 but only in passing — the full elaboration was lost.

**"Not naivety about human behavior."** This phrase appeared in Section 5 v1: "This is not naivety about human behavior — it is recognition that the design of the requirement determines the quality of the response." It was flagged as an "LLM-tell formula" in the v2.1 transformation note and cut. The same phrase appeared in Section 7 v1 and survived to v3.1. Inconsistent treatment of the same formulation across sections.

## 5. Conflicts and Tensions

### Section numbering gap

The drafts go: Introduction (1), Section 2, Section 3, Section 5, Section 6, Section 7, Conclusion (8). There is no Section 4 in the CFP draft set. The Introduction v1 roadmap references "Sections 4 and 5 address institutional conditions for responsible AI-assisted ethics research" — but by v2.1 this becomes "Section 5 addresses conditions for adequate transparency." Section 4 was apparently dropped during the CFP adaptation, but no explicit record of this decision appears in the guidance files examined here.

### Conclusion under-length

CFP_4.4.19 specified 800-1000 words for the Conclusion. The actual v1.1 is ~570 words. The Conclusion's transformation note says it is a redundancy pass that removed ~280 words — meaning the unrecorded v1 was approximately 850 words, within the target range. The redundancy pass brought it below the specified minimum. Whether this was intentional or an artifact of the across-the-board compression is not documented.

### Section 2 missing authenticity addition

CFP_4.4.19 specified adding an authenticity dimension to Section 2's closing paragraph. Section 2 v4.1 does not contain this addition. The transformation note for v4.1 lists what was removed ("Removed duplicate 'philosophically specified account' framing... trimmed mechanism elaborations... removed bolted-on authenticity sentence") — the phrase "removed bolted-on authenticity sentence" directly records the removal of what may have been the CFP_4.4.19 addition. If so, the addition was implemented and then immediately reversed in the same redundancy pass.

### "Not naivety" inconsistency

As noted above, the phrase "This is not naivety about human behavior" was cut from Section 5 v2.1 as an "LLM-tell formula" but retained in Section 7 through v3.1 ("This orientation is not naivety"). The redundancy pass treated the same construction differently in different sections.

### Source model shift

The v1 drafts (2026-03-03 through 2026-03-24) were produced by "Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Claude Code session)." The later versions (2026-04-01 onward) were produced by "Claude Opus 4.6 (Claude Code session)." The model change coincides with the self-expression integration and the redundancy pass. No guidance document discusses this platform shift.
