## 1. Revision Cycles: Depth and Iteration Counts

### 1.1 The Introduction as the most revised section

The Introduction underwent the most revision cycles of any section. The modification log (CFP_4.2.14) records seven entries within the initial session (2026-03-03) alone, followed by a major post-finalization structural revision (2026-03-12), then two further cross-paper revision passes (2026-04-01 and 2026-04-01/02). The initial session already contained: a full draft, two Reviewer B rounds, a Floridi style-sheet pass, an Opus structural rewrite diagnosing "zigzag," user modifications, and citation corrections. The post-finalization revision on 2026-03-12 cut approximately 200 words and rearchitected the cognitivist-objection reply. The double-contestation implementation (2026-04-01) produced Introduction v2 with expanded self-exemplification. The redundancy reduction (2026-04-01/02) then cut the Introduction from approximately 1,270 to approximately 730 words (43% reduction) -- the largest proportional cut of any section.

The Introduction's word count trajectory: approximately 1,060 (initial) -> approximately 950 (post-Opus rewrite) -> approximately 750 (post non-sequitur cut, 2026-03-12) -> approximately 1,270 (post double-contestation expansion, 2026-04-01) -> approximately 730 (post redundancy reduction). The section expanded by roughly 500 words for the double-contestation implementation, then was cut by roughly 540 words in the very next session. This expansion-then-contraction pattern is the most extreme instance of a recurring pattern across the paper.

### 1.2 Section 6 as the most philosophically reworked section

Section 6 went through three numbered draft versions (v1, v2, v3) within its initial adaptation session (2026-03-23), then a complete rewrite of section 6.1 in the double-contestation session (producing v4), then further changes in the redundancy-reduction session. The modification log (CFP_4.2.18) records 18 named modifications (MOD-001 through MOD-018). The Reviewer B record shows: v1 received REVISE, v2 received REVISE, v3 received APPROVE. The philosophical content of section 6.1 was rewritten from scratch at least twice: once during the initial v1-to-v3 cycle (the "two-routes derivation") and once during the double-contestation implementation (replacing the two-routes structure with meta-ethical and ethical routes).

The multi-round philosophical revision on the "ethics-specific paragraph" of section 6.1 is documented in detail (CFP_4.2.18, Reviewer comments): "(a) first revision introduced 'not only because... but also because' formulation -- rejected as introducing parallel argument; (b) second revision identified process-constitutive criteria but treated cognitivist case incorrectly via tracing condition; (c) third revision correctly distinguished two routes, conceding cognitivist output-sufficiency and grounding the requirement in essential contestedness at community level." This three-round philosophical negotiation on a single paragraph is the densest instance of iterative argument refinement in the record.

### 1.3 Section 3: three major structural reconceptions

Section 3 went through three architecturally distinct versions:
- **v1** (2026-03-05): Added "Why Output-Evaluation Fails in Ethics" subsection with a two-step reply (first step: process-dependency; second step: essential contestedness). Approximately 1,410 words.
- **Post-finalization cut** (2026-03-12): First step removed as non sequitur; "two steps" framing removed. Same v1 file edited.
- **v2** (2026-04-01): Double contestation added -- two parallel "routes" (meta-ethical and ethical/authenticity). Parasitism objection addressed. Approximately 1,750 words before redundancy reduction, approximately 1,290 after.
- **v3** (2026-04-03): Two-order restructuring replacing two-route parallel. Expressivism and personal/existential view as first-order conceptions; essential contestedness and Sartrean bad faith as second-order arguments. Williams on integrity added. Sartre added.

Each version represents a fundamentally different argumentative architecture, not a refinement of the same architecture. The modification log (CFP_4.2.23) explicitly states the v2 structure had "an asymmetry: expressivism is a metaethical thesis while the personal/existential tradition is a thesis about philosophical practice. Placing both as 'routes' obscured their logical independence."

## 2. Abandoned Approaches

### 2.1 The "first component" of the cognitivist-objection reply

The most significant abandoned approach is the "first component/step" of the cognitivist-objection reply. It appeared in both the Introduction (as "first component") and Section 3 (as "first step") and argued that output-evaluation in ethics is "already partly process-dependent" because "there is no moral truth-meter" and ethicists assess "whether the right considerations were weighed, the right methods followed, the right sensitivities exercised."

The user identified this as a non sequitur. Opus confirmed: "The cognitivist objection turns on the context-of-discovery/justification distinction -- not on whether we have a moral truth-meter. The objection claims output-level criteria (validity, soundness, whether right considerations are identified) are sufficient. The first component's 'no moral truth-meter' addresses a different question (epistemic access to moral reality)" (CFP_4.7.7, Decision 1).

Crucially, the Opus diagnosis noted that "a cognitivist would simply reply: all those things you describe as 'process-dependent' (identifying right considerations, exercising moral sensitivity) are assessable by reading the paper -- they are output-level criteria." The instinct behind the first component was "not wrong" but it "belongs in Section 6.1 where the thinking-quality argument is properly developed, not as a step in the cognitivist-objection reply" (CFP_4.7.7).

This element had been central to the paper's argument for over a week (2026-03-03 to 2026-03-12) before being cut. Its removal percolated across Introduction, Section 3, work plan, strategic analysis trace, and MEMORY.md. A "do not reintroduce" instruction was added to MEMORY.md. Despite this instruction, the same claim resurfaced in Section 6 v1/v2 as a "discovery/justification paragraph" arguing that "article evaluation always also assessed thinking quality" -- identified by both reviewers as either duplicating the essentially-contested argument "less well" or reintroducing the architecturally excluded first component (CFP_4.2.18, MOD-003).

### 2.2 The cognitivist argument as independent premise

The epistemic trace for the Introduction's argumentative spine (CFP_4.7.5) documents the user proposing a cognitivist/truth-tracking argument: "IF one accepts cognitivism, THEN the institutional test follows straightforwardly." Opus evaluated this and found the inference unsound as stated: "The missing premise is that *thinking quality* matters, not just logical validity -- a cognitivist could still say 'judge the argument, not the process.'" The IF-hedge was also judged unworkable for a published paper.

The user then synthesized the two competing ideas: "What if cognitivism IS the perfect example of an essentially contested feature of moral philosophy?" This reframing dissolved the tension -- cognitivism moved from premise to exhibit, the tonal clash between epistemic humility and confidence dissolved, and the audience-narrowing was eliminated. Opus called this "the strongest version of the argument yet" (CFP_4.7.5, Phase 3). The cognitivism dispute does "triple duty" in the final spine: it generates the objection, illustrates essential contestedness, and is defeated by it.

### 2.3 The reproduction test

The JPEP source version of Section 7 was built around a reproduction test: "Reviewer B attempts to regenerate the intellectual contribution from disclosed inputs, testing sufficiency." This was "already rejected in v3 Section 6" (CFP_4.2.19, Entry 2). The reproduction test was premised on a verification model that the documentation-adequacy model explicitly rejects. Despite being cut, residual reproduction-test language persisted: "verification, replicability" in Section 3 was identified as "a residue of the reproduction-test framing" and removed (CFP_4.2.16, Change 6). The Appendix was flagged as still describing "the v1 reproduction-test architecture" as late as 2026-04-02 (CFP_4.2.22, Note on Appendix).

### 2.4 The virtue ethics bridge (Bridge A)

The integration design trace (CFP_4.7.12) evaluates three "bridge candidates" for grounding the self-expression argument in ethics specifically. Bridge A (virtue ethics: phronesis as character trait, moral sensitivity as epistemic warrant) was assessed as "the strongest candidate" and received a detailed analysis. However, the actual implementation (CFP_4.2.21, double-contestation session) did not use Bridge A at all. Instead, it used expressivism and the authenticity tradition (Socrates/Kierkegaard/Nietzsche) as the two routes. The virtue ethics bridge was effectively abandoned between the design analysis (2026-04-01, SID-20260401-design) and the implementation (2026-04-01, SID-20260401-173934), though no explicit rejection is documented in the implementation log.

### 2.5 The "Requirement of Visibility" subsection

Section 3's "Requirement of Visibility" subsection was deleted during the redundancy-reduction pass as "pure signposting" (CFP_4.2.22). Similarly, Section 5's opening 75-word re-summary of Section 2 was reduced to one sentence, and Section 7's "documentation assessment is learning practice" passage was deleted as duplicating Section 6.3.

### 2.6 The six-move brainstorm

The initial brainstorm for the Introduction spine (CFP_4.7.5, Phase 1) produced six moves. Opus identified only one as load-bearing (Move 5, essential contestedness). The weakest (Move 2, listing ethics-specific methods) was judged "shallow and counterproductive: it implies there is an identifiable set of ethics methods whose judgment-intensiveness can be assessed, which is precisely what the essentially-contested framework denies." Six moves also did not satisfy the user's "stated preference (one deep argument over several shallow ones)."

## 3. Arguments That Changed Direction

### 3.1 The self-expression/authenticity argument: four successive framings

The self-expression argument underwent the most radical directional changes of any argument in the paper:

**Framing 1 (CFP_4.7.11, 2026-04-01):** A meta-philosophical argument applying to all philosophy. "There is a tradition in philosophy that treats the activity not primarily as doctrine-production but as self-expression." The romantic objection is stated and defeated (medium vs. locus of expression). Transparency is the "philosophical hinge."

**Framing 2 (CFP_4.7.12, 2026-04-01):** The integration design identifies the "meta-philosophical gap" -- the argument applies equally to metaphysics, epistemology, mathematics, and literary criticism. Bridge A (virtue ethics) is recommended: "In ethical inquiry specifically, the self-expressive dimension of the research process is not merely a personal or aesthetic value -- it is epistemically significant." The plan: "concentrate, do not distribute widely" -- plant seeds in Introduction and Section 3, develop fully in Conclusion.

**Framing 3 (CFP_4.2.21, 2026-04-01):** The implementation does not use Bridge A. Instead, the argument is distributed across ALL sections (Introduction, Section 2, Section 3, Section 5, Section 6, Section 7, Conclusion) -- the opposite of the design's "concentrate" recommendation. The authenticity tradition (Socrates, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche) becomes the second of two "routes" alongside expressivism. Section 6.1 is rewritten from scratch. The Conclusion gets a full rewrite.

**Framing 4 (CFP_4.2.23/4.7.15, 2026-04-03):** The two-route parallel is replaced with a two-order structure. Expressivism and the personal/existential view become first-order conceptions. Essential contestedness and Sartrean bad faith become second-order arguments. Williams on integrity is added. The personal/existential view is now "a thesis about philosophical practice" distinct from expressivism (a "metaethical thesis"). Essential contestedness is assigned "a single clean role: the primary second-order argument."

### 3.2 The cognitivist objection: from obstacle to exhibit

As documented in CFP_4.7.5, the cognitivist argument started as a competing idea (IF cognitivism, THEN institutional test), was assessed as incompatible with the essentially-contested approach, then was reframed as "the perfect example" of essential contestedness. In the final spine, it does triple duty. This is one of the cleanest instances of a directional change producing a stronger result.

### 3.3 Section 5's derivation: from institutional strategy to philosophical diagnosis

Section 5's three principles (ecological validity, good faith orientation, costly signaling) were originally derived from "discontinuity from prestige systems" -- the argument that opting out of traditional academic metrics creates design space for these principles (CFP_4.2.17, MOD-001). The CFP adaptation rederived them from "the failure of compliance-based mandates." The principles survived; only their justification changed. This is documented as "the dominant transformation" of Section 5.

### 3.4 Section 6.1's traditional-values argument: from tradition to authenticity

In Section 6 v1-v3, the traditional-values paragraph argued: "Philosophy has always valued guided thought [...] intellectual honesty [...] methodological self-consciousness" (CFP_4.2.18, MOD-015). In Section 6 v4 (post-double-contestation), this was replaced: "Primary concern reframed: not detecting inauthentic authors but enabling the authentic technological explorer -- whose authentic mode is AI collaboration -- to present that identity honestly." The justification shifted from "philosophy has always valued X" (appeal to tradition) to "the authenticity argument itself" (principled grounding).

### 3.5 The "confession from imposture" framing

Section 3 v2 initially contained the phrase "the Nietzschean cannot distinguish confession from imposture" (CFP_4.2.16, Post-Finalization Amendment 2026-04-01, Change A). This was replaced because it "frames the authenticity argument as primarily about fraud detection, which inverts the argument's proper emphasis -- the positive concern is enabling new forms of authentic philosophical practice, not policing inauthentic ones." The second problem: "not foreign to philosophical practice" grounded the norm "in existing convention rather than in the authenticity argument itself; tradition is not a justification."

## 4. Unexpected Difficulties

### 4.1 LLM writing tics as persistent problem

The Floridi style sheet (applied in CFP_4.2.14, Entry 3) addressed bold step labels, bold announcement headers, and step numbering as "LLM-signature announcement signals." But LLM tics recurred in later drafts: the redundancy-reduction session (CFP_4.2.22) was triggered by the user instruction to "look for typical LLM tale signs, especially redundancy." The session found five cross-section redundancy patterns, each stated 3-5 times across the paper. The 28% word reduction (approximately 9,165 to approximately 6,630) suggests that LLM-generated prose contained roughly a quarter to a third redundant material even after multiple review rounds.

Specific LLM patterns identified in CFP_4.2.22: "while simultaneously" hedging (Section 2), signposting phrases ("This section addresses the complementary question:"), and multiple verbatim or near-verbatim restatements across sections (e.g., "This objection deserves a serious reply rather than circumvention" appeared in both Introduction and Section 3).

### 4.2 The "prose explosion" in metadata

CFP_4.2.24 documents a different kind of complexity: infrastructure-level prose in frontmatter fields that the graph-building script could not parse. Entries like "Sections I-VI Pattern Summaries and Section Summaries and Modification Logs" had to be "exploded" into individual file IDs. This required chronological reconstruction -- determining which files existed at which session dates -- which itself became research material for the SP-3 phase. A "synthetic node" mechanism was invented to handle references that are real but cannot be represented as files (e.g., "Complete Paper collation, Oct 18").

### 4.3 The cost-structure argument's internal contradiction

In Section 5, the costly-signaling argument initially claimed that "fabricating coherent, comprehensive process records... demands more sophisticated effort than producing honest documentation" (CFP_4.2.17, MOD-002). Reviewer B flagged that this did not acknowledge AI tools could assist fabrication. The user then moderated the asymmetry claim to "roughly as expensive to fabricate as to produce authentically" (MOD-007). But a subsequent sentence ("records specific enough to enable tracing assessment cannot plausibly be generated without having actually engaged in the work they document") directly contradicted the qualification. The user caught this: "wait this is incoherent" (MOD-008). The because-clause was cut.

This is notable as an instance where a philosophical qualification introduced in one editing pass created an internal contradiction that survived into the next editing pass and had to be caught by the user.

### 4.4 Section 6.4's three failed attempts

Section 6.4 was rewritten three times in the v2-to-v3 cycle (CFP_4.2.18, Reviewer A comments): "(a) first rewrite used auditability/legal-evidence framing -- rejected as incoherent with good faith orientation; (b) second rewrite used conversational hedging -- rejected as incoherent with academic register; (c) final version: two observations in academic register." The difficulty was finding a register that explained why AI is used in the documentation process itself without importing framings from incompatible contexts (legal evidence, casual conversation).

### 4.5 The Kierkegaard overreach

Section 3's treatment of Kierkegaard was revised when a "passage claim about 'ethical and religious claims' was also deleted from the text as overreaching (Kierkegaard's argument in CUP is specifically about religious faith; extension to ethics is not established)" (CFP_4.2.16, Post-Finalization Amendment, Change B). This is an instance of a philosophical reference being deployed more broadly than the source text supports.

## 5. Cross-Section Interactions During Revision

### 5.1 The non-sequitur cut as cascading revision

The non-sequitur identification (2026-03-12) is the clearest case of a single diagnosis triggering cross-section changes. The cut of the "first component/step" propagated to: Introduction (first component cut, Gallie paragraph compressed, qualification compressed, tracking pivot updated), Section 3 (first step cut, framing removed, second step reworked, four citations transferred from Introduction), work plan, strategic analysis trace, and MEMORY.md. The trace (CFP_4.7.7) lists 10 files changed in that session.

### 5.2 The double-contestation implementation as whole-paper surgery

The double-contestation implementation (CFP_4.2.21) touched seven sections in a single session: Section 7 (Steps 0 and 3), Section 3 (Step 1), Section 6 (Step 2), Introduction (Step 4), Section 5 (Step 5), Section 2 (Step 6), Conclusion (Step 7). The implementation followed a specific ordering dictated by argumentative dependencies: Section 3 first (where the double contestation is established), then Section 6 (where it is operationalized), then surrounding sections.

The immediately following session (SID-20260401-225323) then performed a 28% word reduction across all the same sections -- suggesting that the implementation pass had introduced substantial redundancy that required immediate corrective editing.

### 5.3 The "Introduction compresses; Section 3 develops" principle

This principle (from the work plan, cited in CFP_4.7.7) governed the interaction between Introduction and Section 3 throughout. After the non-sequitur cut, the Introduction's Gallie paragraph was compressed from approximately 130 to approximately 55 words because "the Introduction and Section 3 were repeating the same content at near-equal length." Four citations (Enoch, Shafer-Landau, Gibbard, Blackburn) were transferred from the Introduction to Section 3 as part of this compression. The principle was applied again in the redundancy-reduction pass: the Introduction's "double-contestation preview" (approximately 120 words) was replaced with 2 sentences, because "Section 3 does the work."

### 5.4 Section 3 to Section 6.1 linkage

The epistemic trace (CFP_4.7.5, Phase 4) identified that "Section 6.1 of the v3 draft already makes the same claim in practical terms ('article evaluation never assessed merely whether arguments are valid -- it always also assessed thinking quality'). Section 3 and Section 6.1 should be explicitly linked in the CFP adaptation." This linkage was implemented but then complicated when the "thinking quality" argument was identified as problematic (the non-sequitur cut). The claim survived in Section 6.1 "where it is properly developed" (CFP_4.7.7) but was removed from Section 3. Later, Section 6.1's discovery/justification paragraph (which made the same claim) was also cut (CFP_4.2.18, MOD-003) as either "an independent argument that duplicates the work of the essential-contestedness argument, less well" or "a reintroduction of the first component."

### 5.5 Section 2 to Section 5 diagnostic chain

Section 5's three principles are rederived from the failure of compliance-based mandates diagnosed in Section 2. This created a dependency: when Section 5.1 re-explained all four Section 2 mechanisms, the user identified it as "very repetitive, considering the essay as a whole" (CFP_4.2.17, MOD-003). The fix was to trust the prior analysis and reference it: "each exploits the interpretive latitude that retrospective, open-ended characterization creates" replaced a full mechanism-by-mechanism list. Section 5.1 went from approximately 300 to approximately 160 words.

### 5.6 Terminology propagation

Terminological changes propagated across sections: "principles" to "conditions" (Section 5 to Section 6, CFP_4.2.18 MOD-008); "editorial judgment" to "evaluative judgment" (Section 7, CFP_4.2.19 Entry 4); "philosophy" to "ethics research"/"ethical inquiry" (pervasive in Section 3 adaptation, CFP_4.2.16 Change 2); "tracking route"/"authenticity route" to "meta-ethical route"/"ethical route" (CFP_4.2.22, MOD-R2). Each required checking consistency across all sections that used the term.

## 6. Apparent Conflicts Between Artifacts

### 6.1 Integration design vs. actual implementation

CFP_4.7.12 (integration design, 2026-04-01) recommends: "Concentrate, do not distribute widely. [...] plant two brief seeds (Introduction, Section 3), and develop the full argument in the Conclusion." The actual implementation (CFP_4.2.21, same date) distributes the argument across ALL seven sections and rewrites Section 6.1 from scratch. The design explicitly warns: "Wide distribution across finalized sections would: disrupt carefully calibrated argument flows; risk redundancy with the process-dependency argument already in Section 6.1; add word count in a paper that is already at or near target length; require reopening sections that have passed dual review." All four of these predicted costs materialized: the redundancy-reduction session (CFP_4.2.22) was needed immediately afterward, cutting 28% of the paper's words.

The design and implementation documents have no explicit acknowledgment of this reversal. They share the same date (2026-04-01) but different session IDs (SID-20260401-design vs. SID-20260401-173934).

### 6.2 Bridge A recommended but not used

CFP_4.7.12 identifies Bridge A (virtue ethics, character-as-epistemic-warrant) as "the strongest candidate" for grounding the self-expression argument in ethics. The implementation (CFP_4.2.21) uses the authenticity tradition (Socrates/Kierkegaard/Nietzsche) instead, without documenting a rejection of Bridge A. The subsequent restructuring (CFP_4.2.23, v2 to v3) adds Williams on integrity and Sartre on bad faith -- neither of which was part of the integration design's analysis.

### 6.3 Section 3 citation location

CFP_4.2.16 (Section 3 log, 2026-03-05) states: "Section 3 names the cognitivism/non-cognitivism dispute but characterises it without adding the full citation set (which belongs in the Introduction where the dispute is first introduced)." But the non-sequitur revision (CFP_4.7.7, 2026-03-12) moves those four citations FROM the Introduction TO Section 3. The original design placed them in the Introduction; the revision placed them in Section 3. The rationale shifted: the Introduction compresses, Section 3 develops, therefore the citations belong where the argument is developed.

### 6.4 Section 6's epistemic virtue paragraph: added then deleted

CFP_4.2.18 MOD-007 adds an "epistemic virtue paragraph" to Section 6.1 per user request, "explicitly flagged as 'not the ground of the requirement.'" CFP_4.2.18 MOD-016 then deletes it: "The paragraph explicitly flagged itself as not providing the ground of the requirement, yet appeared immediately before the MHC framework -- structurally suggesting it was load-bearing." The explicit disclaimer that was meant to prevent misreading was itself identified as evidence that the paragraph did not belong.

## 7. Summary Observations

**Expansion-then-contraction is the dominant revision pattern.** The double-contestation implementation (2026-04-01) added approximately 1,665 words across sections; the immediately following redundancy reduction cut approximately 2,535. Nearly every section went through at least one cycle of being expanded for philosophical reasons and then contracted for prose discipline.

**The user functions as the primary architectural critic.** The non-sequitur identification, the "confession from imposture" reframing, the Kierkegaard overreach correction, the cost-structure incoherence catch, the Section 5.1 repetitiveness diagnosis, and the "this is incoherent" call on Section 5.3 all originated with the user. The AI reviewer (Opus) caught structural issues (zigzag, paragraph ordering, terminological inconsistency) but the deepest philosophical corrections came from the user.

**Abandoned material often reappears in transformed form.** The "first component" (thinking quality is part of evaluation in ethics) was cut from the cognitivist-objection reply but preserved in Section 6.1; it was then cut from Section 6.1 as well. The cognitivist argument was abandoned as a premise but reappeared as an exhibit. The reproduction test was dropped but its residual language persisted across multiple sections.

**The paper's argument changed fundamentally at least three times.** The initial spine (four moves, essentially-contested as central) was established 2026-03-02. The non-sequitur revision (2026-03-12) changed the defeat structure. The double-contestation implementation (2026-04-01) added a parallel argument track. The two-order restructuring (2026-04-03) reconceived the parallel structure. Each change required propagation across multiple sections.

**Infrastructure complexity grew alongside philosophical complexity.** The metadata audit (CFP_4.2.24) documents a different dimension of writing complexity: managing the documentary apparatus itself. Prose references in frontmatter fields, synthetic nodes for unresolvable references, date correction (MM/DD vs DD/MM confusion), and chronological reconstruction to determine which files existed at which session -- these are complexity costs of the transparency methodology itself.
